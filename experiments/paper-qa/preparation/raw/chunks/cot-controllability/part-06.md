## I Other Related Work
CoT reasoning begins as prompting: eliciting intermediate steps improves multi-step reasoning (Wei et al., 2023), including zero-shot think prompts (Kojima et al., 2023). It also serves as a test-time procedure (e.g., self-consistency; reasoning/action/search) (Wang et al., 2023; Yao et al., 2023a,b). More recently, reasoning shifts from prompting to training via process supervision and RL with verifiable rewards (Wen et al., 2025; Havrilla et al., 2024); OpenAI’s o1 is an early production reasoning model (OpenAI et al., 2024). Towards AI safety, CoT monitoring is motivated by the hope that reading a model’s reasoning enables earlier, more granular oversight than output-only checks (Baker et al., 2025; Korbak et al., 2025). Evidence suggests CoT can be highly informative for oversight in practice (METR, 2025; Emmons et al., 2025; Guan et al., 2025).
However, CoT monitoring can be limited by two factors: (i) low monitorability, where training choices reduce the useful signal exposed in CoT (Baker et al., 2025; Skaf et al., 2025; Drori et al., 2025); and (ii) high controllability, where misaligned models manipulate CoT to evade detection—e.g., obfuscating reasoning (Zolkowski et al., 2025), decomposing malicious reasoning into benign-looking steps (Yueh-Han et al., 2025a), or covertly sandbagging under CoT monitoring (Li et al., 2025). While stronger instruction-following within reasoning traces can improve usability and predictability in benign settings (Kwon et al., 2025), our focus is adversarial: under misalignment risk, a model’s ability to flexibly shape its CoT can enable monitor evasion (Zolkowski et al., 2025; Yueh-Han et al., 2025a; Li et al., 2025). Thus, for reliable CoT monitoring, we argue that lower CoT controllability as well as CoT instruction-following ability (holding capability fixed) is preferable. Moreover, the current trend of scaling up models does not ensure safety (Yueh-Han et al., 2025b): without oversight-relevant evaluation, monitorability may remain low and/or controllability high, undermining CoT-based supervision.
