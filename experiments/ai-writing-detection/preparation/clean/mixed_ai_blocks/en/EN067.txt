It is often said that while one may guide a horse to water, even the most persuasive individuals, such as Mr. Lloyd George, cannot compel it to drink. A far more challenging endeavor is to persuade a horse, brimming with health, to feign illness—a realization I recently stumbled upon. This unexpected insight was not something I anticipated; in fact, as I awoke that morning and engaged in my usual routine of contemplating the events that might unfold over the next day, the notion of such equine behavior did not cross my mind at all. However, the observant and curious traveler may encounter many surprising phenomena, and shortly after my lunch, a friend and a taxi appeared seemingly out of nowhere (the divine and the mechanical, if you prefer), whisking me away to a most peculiar establishment that I would never have imagined housing a horse. There, under an uncomfortably bright blue light, I found myself amidst a gathering of individuals as eccentric as the venue itself.

The scene was populated with men in formal attire, casually smoking cigarettes while leaning toward elegantly dressed women; there were a couple of adventuresses, one sporting an extravagant hairstyle that could only belong to someone with a reputation for mischief. A stockman, I suspect, was overly enamored with one of them. Among the crowd was a woman in riding boots, a handsome young man clad in pajamas, and various footmen and page-boys. All appeared to be excessively styled. Who were these people? A newcomer to this bizarre spectacle might wonder. Were they wayward revelers? Perhaps a troupe of masqueraders detained here for photographs and identification? The latter seemed plausible, given the presence of a dignified gentleman with a manuscript who was directing the proceedings. He would occasionally step aside to allow the photographer to operate his camera, yet the words exchanged were lost to me amid the crackling and buzzing emanating from the glaring blue lights.
