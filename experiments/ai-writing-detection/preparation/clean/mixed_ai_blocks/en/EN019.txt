“The location has changed dramatically now... with the influx of visitors, you see,” he would remark, not with disdain, but rather with a sense of melancholy. This was as close as he would come to acknowledging the overwhelming resort town that sprawled along the shore, outshining the sun and drowning out the sound of the waves. However, beyond this clamor—out of earshot from the chaos—lay narrow lanes that felt like secret pathways leading to a tranquil haven. There were squares so filled with silence that stepping into one felt akin to diving into a serene pool. In these peaceful surroundings, my companion and I strolled, discussing Dickens, or more accurately, engaging in what true enthusiasts do: reciting from memory lengthy passages that we both knew by heart. We found ourselves enveloped in the essence of an older England. Fishermen ambled by who could easily have stepped out of a Dickens novel; we ventured into a dusty antique shop and acquired pipe-stoppers intricately carved into characters from The Pickwick Papers. As evening settled over the buildings, casting a golden hue that seemed to saturate everything, we entered the church.

Inside the dimly lit church, my gaze was drawn to the stained-glass windows, which on that clear, golden evening blazed with the vibrant imagery of some of the most intense and passionate expressions of Christian art. Eventually, I turned to my friend and said:

“Do you notice that angel over there? I believe it represents the angel at the tomb.”

He recognized that I was deeply affected, raising his eyebrows in response.

“I suppose,” he replied. “What’s peculiar about that?”

After a brief silence, I asked, “Do you recall what the angel at the tomb proclaimed?”

“I can’t say I do,” he replied, “but where are you rushing off to?”

I hurried him out of the quiet square, past the fishermen’s almshouses, heading toward the coast, while he continued to question my destination with indignation.

“I am off,” I declared, “to insert coins into the automatic machines on the beach. I intend to listen to the musicians. I plan to have my picture taken. I will sip ginger beer straight from its original bottle. I’ll buy a few postcards. I am eager to take a boat ride. I’m ready to enjoy a concertina performance, and if it weren’t for my lack of training, I might even play it myself. I am also willing to ride a donkey; that is, if the donkey is agreeable. I am prepared to be a donkey myself; for all this was instructed to me by the angel depicted in the stained-glass window.”
