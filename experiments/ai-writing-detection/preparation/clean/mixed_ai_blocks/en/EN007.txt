The mist now blankets the foothills, cloaking the upper ridges in a delicate, almost ghostly haze that softens their contours. The snowy slopes are adorned with trees and brush so fine and gentle that they evoke memories of Dürer's engravings, reminiscent of the lion's fur of Saint Jerome and the feathers of a cock depicted in a coat of arms next to a skull. From behind the shroud of the southernmost hill, a distant sound emerges, 

From undiscoverable lips that blow An immaterial horn.

This marks the first subtle hint of the approaching noon train; I linger, eagerly anticipating the next indication. Eventually, I sense its rhythmic pulse, which quiets as the train halts at the flag station nestled beneath the hill. There, the unseen locomotive releases a plume of silver vapor that rises above the mist, forming rounded clouds at its peak, resembling a photograph of a submarine mine explosion—a monumental display of energy frozen in time, a geyser of fine mist that stands like a frosted elm tree. Soon, quick bursts of dark smoke follow, though the sound of their release reaches me only after the train emerges from its ethereal realm, transforming into a mundane reminder of dinner. It bounds across its narrow trestle, leaping over my small river before vanishing between the sandbanks. The mist gradually reclaims its layers, restoring the stillness, and I can hear the melodic creaking of four starlings in an apple tree as they feast on a few decaying apples high in the branches. I turn and glide down the winding paths of the river, avoiding any distractions or embellishments. At the mill-dam, the hockey game continues unabated; the players remain oblivious to the noon train.

Their focus is entirely on a battered disk of hard rubber. As I observe them, I begin to question my earlier assessments, pondering the immense concentration required to fixate on such a mundane object, seemingly eclipsing the calls of hunger and the allure of the surrounding beauty. Is this focus for them akin to the mystic’s "great syllable Om," which facilitates their attainment of Nirvana? I find myself unable to reach that state; all I can do is wonder what the hockey players value that could be even half as precious as everything they overlook.
