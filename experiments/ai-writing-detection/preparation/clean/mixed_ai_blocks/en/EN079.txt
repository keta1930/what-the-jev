"Being perpetually correct can be quite perilous. I've observed that those who arrive late often exude a much more cheerful demeanor than those left waiting. Upon deeper reflection, it became clear to me that Punctualia, despite her self-satisfaction and appearance of propriety, has inadvertently caused numerous misfortunes. The reasoning behind this is quite straightforward. If one is tardy for a train, they inevitably miss it; conversely, if they are not on board when the train meets with disaster, they continue living—only to bear the weight of missed opportunities. I remembered a particularly striking example within my own family. A relative, along with her daughter, had planned a holiday in the Channel Islands. Unfortunately, a cab driver promised to arrive on time but instead only made it across London in a frantic rush, ultimately missing the train by a mere minute. The following day, news broke that the boat to the Channel Islands had capsized at the Casquettes. The two women, filled with relief at their narrow escape, sought out the cab driver to express their gratitude by adopting one of his many children. This true incident serves as a powerful testament to the merits of an anti-punctual stance. Had the women boarded the train, they would have met a tragic fate, while the driver’s child would have faced a future lacking even the basics of education.

Is it any wonder, then, that I briefly considered making a resolution to abandon punctuality altogether in the New Year? Ultimately, I refrained from going that far. Instead, I settled on the intention to be less rigid about timeliness than I previously had been."
