Bunty simply sparkled at us through her tangled white fur, remaining completely motionless.

I’m not particularly accustomed to dealing with unfamiliar dogs; however, given that my hostess had become less mobile, her daughter was driving, and no one else was around, I felt a certain compulsion to suggest that I get out and retrieve the troublesome creature.

"Would you mind terribly?" my hostess inquired. "I assure you she won't bite. She's absolutely delightful."

Despite my efforts to suppress memories of how painful a dog's sudden snap can be, I cautiously approached Bunty, whispering soothing words.

As I got close, she rolled onto her back, raised her paws, and seemingly surrendered her spirit to the heavens; thus, it was easy for me to lift her and carry her back to the trap.

Her owners welcomed her back with enthusiasm, though their reproach was only partially concealed. Just as we were about to resume our journey, an agitated voice urged us to halt, and a stern-looking woman, the type one might describe as a formidable spinster, with a bright red face shaded by a purple parasol, confronted us.

"What," she gasped, "is the meaning of this outrage? How dare you take my dog?"

"Your dog, madam?" I started to respond.

"It’s pointless to deny it," she interrupted, "I witnessed you pick her up and carry her to the trap. This is outrageous. I will report this to the police."

At the same time, one could not overlook that the dog was now displaying signs of joy and recognition that had been absent earlier.

"But—" my hostess began, who typically avoids conflict.

"We should certainly be able to identify our own dog when we see her," the daughter chimed in, unafraid of a confrontation.

"Of course," retorted the irate woman, "if you actually have a dog that belongs to you."
