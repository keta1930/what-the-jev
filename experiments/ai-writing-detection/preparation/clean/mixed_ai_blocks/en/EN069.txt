Let us delve into—well, anything that captures your interest. Take goldfish, for example.

Goldfish represent either a sense of vintage calm or the futility characteristic of the mid-Victorian era, depending on where they are situated within the home. In their natural habitat, from which they have been removed by civilization, they might symbolize daring bravery, steadfastness, or loyalty; however, I cannot assert this definitively. My observations are limited to their presence in the garden or within the confines of the drawing-room. In a serene lily-pad adorned pool, nestled deep within the aged stone terrace, where the blackbird serenades the dawn with its cheerful song, they evoke memories of sundials, lavender, and other cherished relics of the past. Conversely, when they occupy a mundane glass bowl perched on a rickety three-legged table, overshadowed by a cloth-covered canary that maintains a grim silence, they conjure images of antimacassars, horsehair sofas, and an overwhelming sense of gloom. It seems unfortunate that the goldfish has no say in such circumstances. While they appear charming in the pond, it is unlikely that their aesthetic appeal is the reason for their placement there. More likely, it is the human desire to engage with their surroundings that leads to their presence in such settings. Anyone who has sat by the ocean, captivated by its beauty, cannot resist the urge to toss stones into the waves, where the satisfaction of the physical act melds with the visual delight of the splash to create a feeling of pure joy. Similarly, by the edge of the pond, one experiences a similar pull, and since ants' eggs lack the capacity to create ripples and appear messy on the water's surface, the shimmer of gold and silver becomes essential to elevate that enjoyment.

You may not have considered this perspective while feeding the goldfish, but you must have pondered why they seem to favor ants' eggs over all other options. Ants' eggs are arguably the least likely choice one would adopt without some persuasion. It must be a taste developed over time, prompting one to wonder how goldfish came to acquire such a preference.
