The global community has made significant investments of both resources and effort into the education system of England, and the results speak for themselves. The response to Germany's audacious challenge serves as evidence that these investments have been worthwhile. Among all nations, England stands out as the most prepared to harness the power that rightfully belongs to her. While it is essential to acknowledge the distinct strengths of other countries—contributions from Italy, France, our own nation, and even Germany have all played a role—England has emerged with an impressive level of understanding and stability. Although it would be inaccurate to claim that England is flawless—an assertion few Englishmen would make—she has certainly gained extensive knowledge and maintained her composure throughout this journey. Teaching another nation to the same extent is a far more daunting task than the world is willing to undertake, and the likelihood of achieving comparable success seems remote. What England offers the world goes beyond her established institutions and includes the essence of the English freeman, hailing from diverse regions such as Canada, Australia, Africa, or the most distant islands.

This English freeman is remarkably adaptable, capable of performing various tasks across the globe and possessing extraordinary abilities to integrate. Reflect on his roots: he initially embraced his own weight in Danes while remaining true to his identity as an Englishman. He later accepted a Norman ruler, much like he had a Danish one, hoping that the king would not impose too much upon him. However, when William the Conqueror, who lacked understanding of the people, eliminated most of the local leaders and forced the remainder into servitude, how did this resourceful individual manage, in just a couple of centuries, to transform himself and the diverse group of adventurers who accompanied William into refined English gentlemen? From a mix of exiles escaping religious oppression, disinherited younger sons, unemployed craftsmen, opportunists, bondmen, and various others from different backgrounds, he forged the foundation of America. Is this individual likely to lose his way when confronted with age-old challenges in his new homeland? This is a task he has been adeptly navigating for over a millennium. Allow him the time to rediscover his identity in light of these challenges. Do not anticipate a clear and articulate explanation of his actions; he would not be true to the English heritage if he could provide one. Perhaps those in the new leadership positions can assist him in articulating his journey.
