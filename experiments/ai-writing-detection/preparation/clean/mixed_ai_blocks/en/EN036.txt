As we reflect on our past and look ahead, we often yearn for what is unattainable. However, as we progress through life, we gradually find contentment with aspirations that are more grounded. Our hopes and dreams may become more focused, but the vastness of our inner world remains unchanged. We come to realize that the elements of true significance are often much closer than we initially believed. This is akin to a spiritual homecoming. While we have chased after life’s “topless grandeurs,” we ultimately discover that the fulfillment we sought was always easily accessible. By nurturing these intrinsic satisfactions, we uncover an essential truth: the grand ideals we pursue are not external, but rather lie within ourselves.

Yet, I fear I might be sounding preachy, and my intent is not to deliver a sermon. Still, if there were ever a time for some reflection, it is upon reaching the milestone of fifty years. To balance this, let me share a brief anecdote told to me by the current Bishop of Chester during a dinner gathering. This story offers a practical approach to maintaining a youthful heart. In his earlier years, he was connected with Archdeacon Jones of Liverpool, who, at the age of over eighty, had once tutored Gladstone.

One evening, the future Bishop tried to engage the Archdeacon in reminiscing about the distant past. Suddenly, the Archdeacon shifted the conversation, inquiring, “What was the Philharmonic concert like last night?” When the Bishop expressed surprise at this abrupt change, the Archdeacon explained, “Though I am elderly, I wish to keep my heart young, and the best way to achieve that is to focus not on the past but on staying connected to the vibrant life around me.”

It’s important to acknowledge that every phase of life has its own distinct appeals. No single stage can be deemed superior to another, yet I have consistently found myself most enamored with the current phase I am experiencing. At twenty, I believed that age was unparalleled, and now at fifty, my enthusiasm has shifted to this very age. I genuinely feel that there is no better time than now for full enjoyment of life. I am also convinced that if I am fortunate enough to reach sixty, I will surely proclaim that age as the best of all. And why shouldn’t I? There is joy in witnessing the sunlight dance on the morning hills, just as there is in the serene walk home as evening shadows stretch and the coolness of twilight envelops us.
