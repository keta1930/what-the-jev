I recently came across a serious article discussing the outlook for the coming year. This piece featured insights from various industry specialists, including a meteorologist who, unsurprisingly, predicted a rainy summer. It concluded with a summary of the year from Old Moore or perhaps one of the lesser-known prophets. Unfortunately, Old Moore's insights did not resonate with me.

Though I wish I could embrace astrology, I find myself unable to do so. The notion that celestial bodies align perfectly to allow Zadkiel to predict future events is quite grandiose. The thought of a star veering off its natural course just to signal a "sensational divorce case in high life" seems almost absurd. Honestly, I doubt that the stars concern themselves with human affairs. I am not certain what their purpose is or what they are like up close, but a clear night sky would lose its beauty if it were merely a signal for some unsavory financier about an impending crisis in South Africa. While an ordinary person gazes at the vastness of the universe and feels their own insignificance, an astrology believer looks up and is reminded of their own considerable significance. Perhaps it is for the best that I remain skeptical.

Navigating life must be quite challenging for those who are superstitious. Just the other night at dinner, I mentioned that I had never faced the threat of drowning. I’m uncertain whether that statement was entirely accurate, but I believed it was innocuous at the time. Before I could delve deeper into my thoughts, I was abruptly instructed to "touch wood." I argued that my feet were already planted on polished oak and my elbows rested on polished mahogany—one always knew that placing elbows on the table was a habit borne of good instincts. Even so, because one should avoid disputes during meals, I tapped the table a few times, and now, I guess I am supposed to be free from harm. Nonetheless, I am left curious about whom or what I might have appeased in the process.
