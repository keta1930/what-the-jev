"I mentioned that it overwhelmed me as well. While I clung to the foot of the ladder for stability, David opened up to me about his uncertainties regarding enlistment. 

“Most of the guys around here have signed up,” he remarked, “and I feel uneasy about remaining behind. It feels like I’m holding back, you know? It’s not that I wouldn’t want to go; it’s just… (Oh, I just brushed against a wasp!) … there's so many women relying on me. My wife, my mother back in the village, and my aunt; there’s no one else to support them but me. After my duties on the farm, I tend to all three of their gardens and even manage a small allotment down the valley.”

“That’s quite a bit of food you're producing, and that’s important work,” I replied.

His expression brightened at the thought, and he invited me to see the potatoes he had harvested that evening—“a wonderful crop,” as he described them. 

After he finished with the pear tree, he hoisted the ladder onto his shoulder, and we made our way to David’s garden in the village. There, I noticed his potatoes, some spread out to dry after being harvested, while others were neatly packed in sacks. Additionally, he had marrows, beans, cabbages, and lettuces. With a hint of embarrassment, he offered me some of the largest potatoes—“just a little hobby,” he explained, suggesting it was merely a small gesture.

As I walked away into the dimming evening, my hands laden with potatoes, I encountered the proprietor of the Blue Boar, his shirt sleeves rolled up as usual, revealing his robust, tanned arms. 

“Have you heard the bad news about Mrs. Lummis?” he inquired, glancing toward the cottage across the road. 

“What happened?” I asked, thinking of her son. It had been two months since we last heard from him. 

“Sadly, Jack has been reported killed near La Bassée in June. Such a nice fellow—and her only child.”

Then, shifting to a more positive note, he added, “Jim is coming back tomorrow. He’ll be getting his officer’s uniform ready and will finally have a break—the first one since he left a year ago.”

“You must be excited to see him,” I said.

“Absolutely,” he replied, beaming widely."
