In my youth, I encountered one of those influential figures who helped shape the Empire—a man clad in an astracan coat, complemented by a tight, curly, black moustache. It remains uncertain whether he adorned himself with the moustache as a deliberate choice or if his commanding presence allowed him to cultivate it in an extraordinary manner, perhaps even causing tiny moustaches to sprout on his garments. I distinctly recall him imparting a piece of wisdom: “A man can’t get on nowadays by hanging about with his hands in his pockets.” In a moment of youthful irreverence, I retorted that perhaps success came from having one’s hands in others’ pockets. This prompted him to delve into a discussion about Moral Evolution, suggesting that my flippant remark held some validity. This episode has lingered in my memory, now intertwining with a recent experience I had.

I can count on one hand the instances in which I have pilfered, and the one time I did so was, amusingly, an act of absent-mindedness where I ended up picking my own pocket. This mischief can be aptly described as such, as retrieving items from my own pocket evoked the same thrill and anxiety one might associate with theft; I was filled with a mix of ignorance and curiosity about what I might uncover. While it might be a stretch to label myself as particularly organized, I do typically have a clear understanding of where all my belongings are—provided they remain outside the deep recesses of my pockets. Once anything gets lost within those dark depths, I bid it a melancholic farewell, much like Virgil’s sentiment. I assume that the items I have inadvertently consigned to my pockets remain there, much like the treasures lost to the sea. I hold both these bottomless pits in equal regard, marked by a respectful ignorance. They say that on the final day, the ocean will release its secrets, and I imagine that at that moment, an array of peculiar items will emerge from my pockets as well. However, I have completely forgotten what those items might be, and aside from the cash, I wouldn’t be surprised to find anything at all among them.
