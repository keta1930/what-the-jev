He had only been in Paris for about an hour when—let me recount this tale as my travel companion shared it with me.

"I can't speak for your experiences in Paris," he remarked, "but I’ve been taken advantage of at every turn."

As he stood up, I remained comfortably reclined in my berth, observing his struggles in the cramped room, and I found myself disliking his undershirt.

"I had only just arrived in Paris when I needed to settle a fare with a cab driver," he continued. "I offered him a franc. He scrutinized it, chuckled, and handed it back to me. I tried again with another franc, only to receive the same treatment. Eventually, I managed to find some legitimate currency to pay him off and settled down at a café to recall where I had received the change that had resulted in these worthless coins. Much of my week in Paris was spent in similar pursuits."

He sighed as he pulled on his trousers, the red suspenders standing out brightly against his attire.

"I showed the counterfeit francs to a waiter," he elaborated, "and he reacted just like the cab driver, laughing. In fact, aside from nudity, nothing seems to amuse Parisians more than a bad coin. The first reaction from everyone who saw my collection was laughter." His expression soured with anger. "This carefree indifference toward a matter that reflects a complete lack of integrity in human dealings," he asserted, "illustrates the level of cynicism that Parisians have reached."

I found myself in agreement with his assessment.
