The innovative and daring tobacconist, Mr. Godall, upon his return to London—so reminiscent of Baghdad—would need to find a fresh base of operations. Perhaps he might consider establishing an oyster-bar, reminiscent of the one near Leicester Square where a young man once offered cream tarts; however, an American bar might be more fitting. Yet, if Mr. Godall truly wished to witness human behavior in its rawest and most spontaneous form—especially during the nighttime hours—he would likely opt for a coffee-stall. After dark, the vendors at these stalls become the most genuine allies of the less fortunate. There is a coffee-stall just a stone’s throw from my home, and regardless of when I arrive, its proprietor is always lively and cheerful, serving the hottest drinks imaginable to those with hesitant hearts. Surrounding his stall are thirsty and hungry nightlife enthusiasts, predominantly soldiers, often accompanied by lovely companions. Everyone addresses him by his first name, and a spirit of camaraderie fills the air as laughter and conversation abound. Whenever I awaken in the middle of the night, regardless of the unsettling dreams that may have disturbed my slumber, I find comfort in knowing that the familiar world remains intact. The jovial voices and laughter from the coffee-stall assure me that I have not drifted into some other realm. "Good," I think to myself, "I am still here." It would indeed be a delightful experience—and a testament to a life well-lived—to joyfully serve so many unsung heroes during those late hours, while also providing reassurance to restless neighbors of the world’s enduring stability.
