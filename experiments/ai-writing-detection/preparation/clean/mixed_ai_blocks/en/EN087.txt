Arriving late, he quickly set about his duties. Each individual needed to be acknowledged personally, and some came with specific concerns seeking guidance; others were looking for comfort in their grief; most, like myself, had saint medals that they hoped would be further blessed. Additionally, a few priests brought along elderly parishioners who, though not affluent, would cherish the occasion more if it included a bit of friendly exchange. Thus, our host had a significant task ahead, yet he approached it with commendable tact. When he finally reached me, he offered no words, but my friend from the traditionalist faction presented the situation of a dying young man and received enough reassurance to feel at ease. Meanwhile, I noticed an elderly gentleman dressed in black, holding a black bag, who was scanning the walls from the entrance, later revealed to be a doctor there to revive those who faint from the overwhelming nature of the ceremony.

Upon concluding his visits, our host moved to the center of the room and gave a brief speech about the significance of his blessing and the necessity of moral integrity. He then offered us one last collective blessing before departing, prompting us to attempt to regain our upright positions, with the older ladies finding the support of their attending priests quite beneficial during this effort.

My own knees ached considerably, but I paid it no mind; I had just witnessed Pope Benedict XV.
