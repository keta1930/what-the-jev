In a recent moment of reflection, I likened the swaying of trees and the unseen forces of the wind to the dynamics of the world around us, illustrating how the tangible is influenced by the intangible. This analogy arose quite simply as I found myself composing the article amidst a wooded setting. However, as I return to Fleet Street—a place I find, with a hint of bias, more enchanting and poetic than any forest—I find this spontaneous metaphor lingering in my thoughts. The figures of people around me resemble a vast woodland, with their spirits akin to the invisible breeze. Each individual who engages with me seems to embody this surreal quality, akin to the edges of a forest silhouetted against the horizon. The man conversing with me? He can be seen as nothing more than a speaking tree. The delivery driver gesturing frantically for me to clear the path appears as a collection of branches, animated by an unseen force, a natural entity I can regard with tranquility. Similarly, the officer raising his hand to caution oncoming buses about the potential risk posed by my presence can be perceived as a bush momentarily disturbed by the gust of societal order, a force that surpasses chaos. Over time, the woodland imagery begins to fade. Yet, the stark juxtaposition between what is visible and what lies beyond our sight—the profound realization that faith in the unseen outweighs the observable—suddenly resurfaces with striking clarity. Just as Fleet Street becomes more familiar, and thus more dazzlingly confusing, a vivid violet poster catches my eye, boldly proclaiming: “Should Shop Assistants Marry?”
