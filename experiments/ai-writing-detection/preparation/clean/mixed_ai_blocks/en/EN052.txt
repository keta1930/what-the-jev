However, this is not the essence of youthful infatuation. The genuine experience of young love arrives with the first flutter of emotions. While many chuckle at the term "calf love," it is akin to marveling at the beauty of dawn or the arrival of springtime. When David Copperfield found himself enamored with the eldest Miss Larkins, he was not simply attracted to her; rather, he was captivated by the vast possibilities the universe offered, and she was merely the conduit for his feelings.

It is crucial to experience "calf love" during one's youth. This type of affection is similar to childhood illnesses like measles—generally benign in youth but potentially problematic in adulthood. The "calf love" expressed by an older individual often leads to complications. This is reflected in the saying, "There's no fool like an old fool." For this reason, an older person should approach love with caution. They ought to evaluate the situation thoughtfully, much like Mr. Barkis did. This wise character approached the matter of love with a serious demeanor:

"'So she makes,' Mr. Barkis mused after pondering for a while, 'all the apple pastries, and does all the cooking, does she?'

I confirmed that this was indeed the case.

"'Well, I’ll tell you something,' Mr. Barkis continued. 'Perhaps you might be writing to her?'

"'I certainly will write to her,' I assured him.

"'Ah!' he remarked, slowly shifting his gaze toward me. 'If you are writing to her, perhaps you could also mention that Barkis is willing, would you?'"

This exchange exemplifies a careful approach to romance in middle age. The error made by the "Northern Farmer" was applying this same level of caution to youthful pursuits. He advised his son Sammy against marrying solely for wealth, suggesting instead to seek out where money could be found, as he had done in his own life.

He recounted his own experience: "And I went where money was, and your mother came to me with plenty of savings and a nice piece of land. Perhaps she wasn’t a beauty; I never gave it a second thought. But was she as delightful to cuddle and kiss as any lass? Certainly!"

I have always wished that Sammy disregarded his father's advice and chose to pursue the poor parson's daughter instead.
