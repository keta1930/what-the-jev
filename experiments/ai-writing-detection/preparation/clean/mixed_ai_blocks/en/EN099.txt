What is it that we all seek? I believe it lies surprisingly close. As a child, I imagined that Heaven or some form of Fairyland was situated just behind me, which is why I could never catch a glimpse of it, no matter how often I turned around in an attempt to surprise it. This led me to envision a man endlessly spinning on one foot, much like a top, trying to find that elusive world that perpetually evaded him. Perhaps this is the reason the world spins. It might be that the world is eternally glancing over its shoulder, striving to reunite with what perpetually slips away, yet is essential for its very existence.

In any event, as I have mentioned, I believe that we must always envision our ultimate aspirations as being curiously close. Science boasts of the vast distances of stars and the incredible remoteness of its subjects. In contrast, poetry and religion emphasize the startling nearness and almost threatening immediacy of their matters. The Kingdom of Heaven is always described as “At Hand,” while Looking-glass Land lies just beyond the mirror.

Consequently, I would not find it astonishing if a sudden turn on a street led me to the center of that intricate labyrinth where all mystics wander. It wouldn’t surprise me in the least if I turned a corner on Fleet Street and encountered an even stranger lamp; nor would I be shocked to find myself in Elfland after another turn.

While such occurrences would not baffle me, I recently found myself taken aback by something far more surprising. As I stepped off Fleet Street, I unexpectedly discovered myself in England.

The peculiar shock I felt perhaps warrants some explanation. Even during the bleakest or most challenging times, there is a fundamental truth about our nation that should never be overlooked. This can be succinctly expressed: England is not nearly as foolish as it appears. The outward representations and stereotypes associated with England often mislead those who encounter them. This country is characterized by an oligarchic structure, and it has a tendency to prefer that its oligarchy remains inferior to the populace itself.
