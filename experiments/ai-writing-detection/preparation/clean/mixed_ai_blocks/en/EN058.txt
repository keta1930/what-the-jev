"According to my friend, it seems to be my fate to purchase items at exorbitant prices and then, if I manage to sell them at all, to do so at the lowest possible rates. More often than not, when I grow weary of a painting or decorative piece, I find myself practically compelled to give it away; the act of having someone accept it feels like I’m asking for a personal favor. However, the other day marked an unusual deviation from this trend, leading me to wonder if perhaps my fortunes have shifted and I am finally meant to become a truly fortunate dealer.

This incident unfolded during my meanderings through the antique shops of a historic cathedral city, where I stumbled across a portfolio filled with watercolors. Among these, one caught my eye that I believed might be a Turner, especially since a prior owner had also shared that belief, inscribing the illustrious name along with its initials—often misarranged—beneath the artwork.

“What’s the price for this one?” I inquired with a hint of skepticism.

The dealer responded, “Well, if it were an authentic Turner, it would command a fortune. But let’s say ten shillings. You can have it for that price; though frankly, I’m indifferent if you don’t buy it, as I plan to take it to London next week for a professional opinion.”

I considered his words carefully.

“Just to be clear, I can't guarantee its authenticity,” he added.

I handed over the ten shillings.

The astonishing details of how I managed to sell the drawing for fifty pounds are not necessary here; the crux of this story lies not in negotiating with collectors, but rather in reconciling with my own conscience. The remarkable truth is that I netted a profit of forty-nine pounds ten and felt a surge of joy. Then, my thoughts began to turn.

The dealer, who operated that shop on the narrow street by the cathedral's west door, deserved a share of this unexpected fortune. He treated me fairly, and it only seemed just that I should treat him fairly in return. Giving him half of the earnings felt like the right thing to do."
