A little over a month ago, as I was preparing to depart from London for a vacation, a friend entered my Battersea flat and discovered me amid a chaotic scene of partially packed bags. 

“You seem to be heading off on an adventure,” he remarked. “Where are you off to?” 

With a bag strap clenched between my teeth, I responded, “To Battersea.” 

“The humor in your statement eludes me,” he replied. 

“I am indeed going to Battersea,” I clarified, “to Battersea via Paris, Belfort, Heidelberg, and Frankfort. My comment was not meant to be humorous. It was simply factual. I plan to traverse the globe until I find Battersea once again. Somewhere amidst the sunsets or sunrises, within the far reaches of our planet, lies a small island I yearn to discover: one adorned with gentle green hills and towering white cliffs. Travelers say it goes by the name of England (though Scottish travelers refer to it as Britain), and there are whispers of a lovely place at its core named Battersea.” 

“I suppose it’s unnecessary to point out,” my friend said, attempting to adopt an air of intellectual superiority, “that this is Battersea?” 

“Indeed, it is utterly unnecessary,” I replied, “and spiritually inaccurate. I cannot perceive any semblance of Battersea here; I cannot see London or even England. I fail to notice that door or that chair because a veil of slumber and habit has clouded my vision. The only path to rediscover them is to venture elsewhere; this, after all, is the true purpose of travel and the genuine joy of vacations. Do you really think I travel to France to experience France? Do you believe I visit Germany to explore Germany? I will certainly find enjoyment in both places, but they are not what I seek. My quest is for Battersea. The essence of travel is not merely to set foot on foreign soil; rather, it is to eventually view one’s homeland as if it were foreign. Now, I must caution you that this Gladstone bag is both compact and heavy, and should you dare utter the term ‘paradox,’ I may just throw it at you. I did not create this world, nor its paradoxes. It is not my fault, but rather a reality, that the only route back to England is to first depart from it.”
