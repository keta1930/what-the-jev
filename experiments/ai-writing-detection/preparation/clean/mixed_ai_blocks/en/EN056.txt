The underlying cause of the slight gloom that had settled upon me was the lost hawthorn. You might call me a sentimentalist for clinging to such trivial notions in this harsh reality filled with suffering. Yet, it is perhaps this very harshness that renders those fleeting thoughts so deeply affecting. Through these musings, one becomes aware of the ephemeral nature of existence. The cycles of nature—its arrivals and departures—are intertwined so intricately that we can never truly ascertain whether the joy of one outweighs the sorrow of the other. It is perpetually both "Hail" and "Farewell" in a single exhale. Today, I heard the cuckoo's call echoing across the meadows, and I discerned a wavering in his second note. Before long, that note will fade away entirely, leaving only a solitary call resonating through the valley, reminiscent of spring's curfew bell.

Who among us would not wish to capture these ephemeral moments of beauty if given the chance? Who would not desire the cuckoo's twin calls to linger endlessly over the sunlit fields, or want the blackbird to continue its melodic gratitude long after the summer rains? How can one observe the daffodils swaying in joyful dance without echoing Herrick's timeless lament about the brevity of that dance:—

Fair daffodils, we weep to see You haste away so soon; As yet the early-rising sun Has not attain'd its noon. Stay, stay. Until the hasting day Has run But to the evensong; And, having prayed together, we Will go with you along.

Indeed, I believe Herrick would have pardoned my brief moment of nostalgia regarding the white hawthorn. He would have grasped my feelings. You would recognize this empathy if you reflect on the second stanza, which, if you are who I believe you to be, you can recall without needing to consult any text.

This same awareness of beauty's impermanence inspired the "Ode to a Grecian Urn," where the essence of pastoral beauty is immortalized in a state of eternal bliss:—

Ah, happy, happy boughs! That cannot shed Your leaves, nor ever bid the spring adieu.
