Indeed, a beloved flower must undoubtedly be one that blooms in spring. This gives you several options: the daffodil, violet, primrose, and crocus. The bluebell arrives too late in the season, while the cowslip is merely a lackluster version of the primrose. Other flowers like camellias and anemones belong to an entirely different category. So, will you favor the violet or the crocus? Alternatively, will you take inspiration from the legendary Disraeli and choose primroses for your statue?

As someone who spends the majority of my time in London, I find that the violet, primrose, and crocus all share a common drawback—they do not lend themselves well to being picked. My ideal flower needs to enhance my home; to truly showcase its beauty indoors, it must possess a long stem. The crocus, in particular, is hardly suited for harvesting. While I appreciate its allure as the initial sign of spring gracing our parks, I desire it closer to home. Picking a crocus only to place it in water is a lost cause, and it would be cruel to uproot the primrose and violet from their natural environments. In contrast, the daffodil eagerly invites picking. It is precisely what it awaits.

“Long stalks, please.” What person, tasked by a partner to gather flowers for the home, hasn’t heard this request? And can any other flower's stem rival the daffodil’s in terms of length, strength, and beauty? Unlike many blooms that require foliage to complement them, daffodils can stand proudly on their own in a vase, with their vibrant green and yellow hues bringing the essence of spring into any space. A home adorned with daffodils radiates warmth, irrespective of the weather outside. Daffodils in a green bowl can brighten even the bleakest winter day.
