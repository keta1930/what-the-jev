"Who has spent the night alone to hear the wild goose call?"

Occasionally, there comes a moment when you find yourself awake in the stillness of the night. The reason behind this phenomenon eludes me, as it seems unrelated to any discomfort from overindulgence in food, an excess of caffeine, or the excitement of an eventful day. You typically settle in with the hope of enjoying a restful night. Yet, almost instantly, the subtle sounds of the forest amplify, merging into the expansive hush that accompanies the onset of sleep; suddenly—snap!—you find yourself fully alert.

It could be that your reservoir of energy is brimming, or perhaps, more intriguingly, the great Mother calls you forth to explore the deeper mysteries of her domain.

Unlike typical insomnia, being awake at night in the woods has its own charm. The frantic desire to fall asleep fades into a state of blissful apathy. You begin to care less about the need for rest. Your thoughts float in a dreamy suspension, where judgment and reflection are softened. Sensory impressions drift in and out of your awareness. Occasionally, they present themselves clearly for contemplation, while at other times, they dissolve into the haze of near-sleep. They gently caress the drowsy imagination, allowing you to sense the vastness from which they emerge. Your faculties receive this serene ambiance. The senses of hearing, sight, and smell become extraordinarily acute to the sounds and scents that dance through the night air, yet your ability to actively appreciate these sensations drifts into a peaceful slumber, as if they were delicate rose petals.

In these moments, you might hear what the voyageurs refer to as the voices of the rapids. Remarkably, many people go their entire lives without perceiving them. They emerge softly, distinctly beneath the tumultuous sounds of rushing water, even amid the lighter gurgles and tinklings that often overshadow them. They resemble fleeting images that flit across your field of vision, vanishing the instant you focus your gaze, only to reappear enchantingly when you allow your mind to wander again. In that tranquil state of hazy awareness, the voices emerge; yet, when you attempt to listen closely, they recede, leaving only the boisterous cascades and gentle murmurs behind.
