"I find it difficult to define what beauty signifies to her, as one cannot perceive her features beyond her essence. It's akin to how birds overlook the window frame when they gaze at the vast sky.

Nonetheless, beauty can also be viewed from a starkly pragmatic perspective. Imagine if humanity had evolved with a single eye, positioned centrally on the forehead; individuals with two eyes might be regarded as grotesque, much like a hand composed solely of thumbs. We would attend exhibitions featuring the two-eyed individual with the same mindset that we currently have when observing a bearded woman. Our interest would not stem from admiration for his dual vision, just as we do not admire the beard; rather, we would be drawn by a curious sense of disgust at his peculiar condition and a reassuring relief that we had escaped such a plight. We would behold our solitary eye with a sense of pride, convinced we embodied the true standard of beauty, while viewing the two-eyed figure before us as a bizarre deviation from the norm.

Ultimately, beauty serves as a tribute to our practical needs. In the struggle for survival, humanity has found it advantageous to possess two eyes for depth perception, just as having four fingers and one thumb proves more functional than having five thumbs. Our anatomical features have evolved in ways that best equip us for the challenges we face. The more effectively they serve this fundamental purpose, the more we ascribe beauty to them. Consequently, our perceptions of beauty are not absolute but rather dependent on context. The necessity of two eyes enhances our ability to navigate the world, leading us to cherish them even more, with our affection growing in relation to their functional perfection.

I observe that Peggy, cradling her one-eyed cat in the sunlight, has yet to embrace our conventional standards of beauty. However, she will likely conform to these norms as her dresses grow longer."
