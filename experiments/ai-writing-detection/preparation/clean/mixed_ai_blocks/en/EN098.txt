The essence of any profession can be viewed as either noble or base, depending on whether it allows individuals to pursue objectives beyond their own personal gain. Take a doctor, for instance; while he charges fees from patients, his true aim lies in alleviating suffering. Similarly, a schoolteacher earns a wage through their educational endeavors, yet their motivation transcends mere self-interest as they champion the cause of learning. Artists, regardless of their chosen medium, convey messages to society, sharing their interpretations of truth; while they may benefit personally, their contributions go beyond simple profit. These professions, among countless others, embody a spirit of nobility. When we embark on these paths, we often do so with lofty ambitions, vowing to uphold the ideals we hold dear and resisting the temptation to compromise our values for personal benefit. However, in many instances, we fall short. The artist may find that creating commercial pieces like "Mother's Darling" becomes a priority, resulting in a lack of creativity. The physician may prioritize a pleasing bedside manner over engaging in research. The educator may abandon innovative teaching philosophies to align with the expectations of their superiors. Although we stumble, the fault does not lie in the inherent nature of the profession; we have access to opportunities that remain available. The guiding light of our ideals continues to shine for those willing to seek it.

Turning our attention to the legal profession, one might question whether a barrister is motivated by anything beyond self-serving ambitions. What ideals does he pursue? It is not my concern whether he succeeds or fails in reaching those aspirations; rather, I am curious about his ultimate goals. In moments of solitude, when we are stripped of external validation, we may find ourselves reflecting on our true impact—not on our financial gains or rising fame, but on the good we contribute to society. If a barrister encounters such a moment of introspection, his only solace may come from knowing he plays a role in upholding Justice. Should he take pride in his profession and find tolerance for himself during these quiet reflections, it is essential that he believes he is contributing to the affirmation of legal rights and the rectification of legal wrongs. However, this is not sufficient on its own. Just as a physician, through greater knowledge and experience, enhances their ability to combat diseases—thereby advancing both their career and the medical field—so too must a teacher, who learns improved methods of instruction to provide superior education, thereby enriching the pool of knowledge. In a similar vein, a barrister must recognize that increasing his expertise as an advocate not only benefits his career but also enhances his ability to contribute to the fair administration of Justice, which remains his ultimate ideal.
