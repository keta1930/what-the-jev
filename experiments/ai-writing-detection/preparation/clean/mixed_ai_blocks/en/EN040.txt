"Ah," I murmured faintly, "I guess it's because he earns his living that way. Now I…" "Now you," he interjected, "do the same, as you wish for your fruit trees to yield fruit, for your roses to flourish, and for your cabbages to thrive. Who could be more ruthless than you when dealing with slugs and other creeping or flying nuisances? No, no, we are all striving to survive—yourself just as much as the spider, and the spider just as much as the fly." "We are all Huns," I lamented. "What a loathsome world it is." "Not at all," he countered. "It’s quite a cheerful world, in fact. I raise my glass to the health of the spider."

"And you feel no compassion for the fly?" I queried. "Not in the slightest," he answered. "I stand firmly for what is right." "Whose version of right is that?" I pressed. "Mine," he declared. "Each of us must act based on our own perspective. That’s the nature of the greenfly, just as it is for the spider. We will never be able to align all viewpoints in this world. The struggle for existence will continue indefinitely. At times, the greenfly may prevail; at other moments, the spider will. Take a look at that cherry tree in the orchard. Just a month ago, its boughs were heavy with fruit. Now, not a single cherry remains. The blackbirds and starlings have stripped it bare. From their perspective, those cherries exist solely for them, and they are justified in their actions. They are unaware of the property laws created by humans for their own security. Attempting to show them your title-deeds or remind them of law enforcement and theft regulations is futile. Our moral framework is designed for us alone, not for them.

"We are all products of our unique viewpoints," he continued. "Consider Jones from next door; before he purchased a car, he harbored intense disdain for drivers—he referred to them as road-hogs, advocated for taxing these ‘land-torpedoes’ out of existence, and expressed deep concern for children coming home from school. Now, he drives as aggressively as anyone else; he toots the loudest horn and complains about how parents let their children play in the streets. The only change is his perspective.
