"After being deprived of a particular enjoyment for several years, one often discovers that upon returning to it, the experience is not as pleasurable as one had envisioned. During the time of absence, an idealized image tends to form, leading to the realization that the actual experience is rather mundane. It may still be enjoyable, yet it lacks the extraordinary qualities one anticipated. Many of us have come to understand this reality during the recent months of peace. We have engaged in activities we longed to partake in during the war, and while they have been quite enjoyable, they do not live up to the dreams we crafted while in France and Flanders. Moreover, the simple joys of civilian life, such as the absence of saluting or medical board appointments, quickly lose their initial charm.

However, I was fortunate enough to experience a pre-war delight this week that did not disappoint. My visit to Lord’s to watch first-class cricket was as exhilarating as I had hoped it would be. 

There are individuals who seek to "enhance cricket," much like a certain director who once reviewed a play I submitted. He politely claimed to have enjoyed reading it, but then proceeded to highlight its flaws. For instance, he noted, “You have two brothers here. They shouldn’t be brothers; they should be strangers instead. Then, one of them marries the heroine, which is incorrect; it should be the other brother who wins her hand.” He continued to critique Aunt Jane, suggesting she was rather dull, and proposed an arrest for bigamy in the second act. He even suggested relocating the fourth act to Monte Carlo, adding a subplot of blackmail. I understood his perspective but pointed out that he wasn’t truly critiquing my work; rather, he was constructing an entirely different narrative—one that I had no intention of writing myself."
