A rather gloomy individual. Many consider him a difficult companion. A few nights ago, while dining in London, I spoke with a woman who owns a home in Sussex, and she revealed that she hadn’t visited it in quite some time.

"I used to tolerate the owl," she remarked, "but ever since the war, he has become unbearable. His hooting at night fills me with despair to the point that I feel I might lose my sanity."

"And so, you choose to listen to the owl in London?" I inquired.

"The owl in London?" she echoed.

"Indeed," I replied, "the owl that hoots in Carmelite Street and Printing House Square."

"Ah," she said, "but that owl is quite ridiculous. The one down in the countryside, however, carries a much more serious demeanor."

"He expresses very foolish ideas with such gravity," I interjected.

"Yes, but in the quiet and the dark, there seems to be no response to him," she countered.

"My dear lady," I suggested, "if you gaze up at the stars, you might discover a rather complete answer."

I must admit that I find the owl not only bearable but also invigorating. Listening to the pessimist freely express his thoughts is quite refreshing. It is the vague and undefined anxieties that paralyze us, but when my owl articulates the bleakest perspective, I begin to feel uplifted and ready to confront challenges. Is this truly the worst that can be said? If so, then let’s explore the best possible outcomes and strive to achieve them. "That’s impossible," a pessimist once told Cobden. "Indeed," Cobden replied, "then we should start addressing it as soon as possible." 

Oh, oh, I tell my owl, everything is lost, is it? Just wait for dawn and listen to what that little fellow in the red waistcoat has to say. He has an entirely different narrative to share, one that is far more plausible than yours. I shall retire for the night, leaving you to your lamentations among the trees until the sun rises and reveals what a dismal deception you truly are.
