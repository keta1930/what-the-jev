"While I have yet to fully explore the topic of lunch in London (and I must note that I will not have the opportunity to indulge today), let’s take a moment to reflect on lunch in the countryside. I am not referring to dining al fresco, as it is evident that there is no meal quite as delightful as one enjoyed outdoors; this is a well-known truth that requires no elaboration in a brief piece like this. Instead, I am focusing on the experience of having lunch at a country residence. One of the most enjoyable aspects of lunch in such a setting is the freedom to sit next to whomever you wish. Unlike dinner, where one may inadvertently end up seated beside the wrong person, or breakfast, where timing is crucial to secure a spot beside someone special, lunch offers a straightforward solution—you simply follow her in and find your place.

However, there exists a unique situation that transcends both London and the countryside, one that encapsulates the essence of an enjoyable lunch more than any other setting. This realization was sparked by a recent experience that inspired my thoughts on lunch. I am speaking of lunch on a train! Ideally scheduled around 1:30 PM, this mealtime occurs when you are sufficiently distanced from London and feeling hungry. As the stunning scenery whizzes by—crossing rivers, gliding past quaint villages, and moving slowly through historic towns—the waiter brings your meal, placing the potatoes conveniently next to you before discreetly departing.

At this point, the waiter is taking a chance. Here’s my take: if a man truly enjoys his potatoes, he must possess a solid character."
