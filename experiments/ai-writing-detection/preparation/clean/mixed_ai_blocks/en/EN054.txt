As time passed, the expected happened. "Do you think," my companion queried, "that this gentleman resembles Dr. Sullivan from Harley Street, who visited last summer?"

"What Dr. Sullivan are you talking about?" asked the newcomer.

"The Dr. Sullivan from Harley Street, who went fishing here last summer. Don’t you recall? He looks just like this man."

"The only Dr. Sullivan I’m familiar with," the newcomer replied, "is Dr. Sullivan from Newcastle. He’s quite elderly now and a very knowledgeable individual. He owns an incredible private museum. He—"

"No, no, the Dr. Sullivan I’m referring to was from Harley Street—a specialist—who fished at the Manor last summer and stayed in the hotel."

"Dr. Sullivan of Newcastle is much older than this gentleman," the other insisted, "and he doesn’t resemble him at all. He’s a fascinating character. He’s a leading expert on the South Sea Islanders. You should see his collection of Fijian war clubs."

"That’s not the Dr. Sullivan I’m thinking of. You must remember him," my friend pressed; "we gathered every evening, just as we are now—Dr. Sullivan of Harley Street, the specialist, a clean-shaven man, exactly like this gentleman here. Everyone has noticed the resemblance."

"Dr. Sullivan of Newcastle has a beard," the newcomer countered. "And he is very old by now. A vast reservoir of eclectic knowledge. He once showed me his collection of coins and medals. His coins date back to the Roman Emperors, and he has fascinating stories about each one. His collection—"

"Yes, but—"

"—of idols is astonishing. You’ve never seen such humorous figures that the natives worship. He collects everything. He even has a mummy adorned with blue beads. He possesses skulls from all corners of the globe, showcasing various formations. It’s been several years—"

"Yes, but—"

"—since I last saw him, and of course he may very well be—"

"Yes, but—"

"—deceased. However, if he is still alive, he’s someone worth meeting. If you ever travel to Newcastle, sir," he directed this toward me, "make sure to seek him out. But he must be quite old by now. He—"

At that moment, I completed my drink and excused myself to retire for the night. As I undressed and glanced in the mirror, a smile crept across my face at the image looking back at me. "You can enjoy a better night’s sleep," I remarked, "for there are indications that you are about to experience some rest."
