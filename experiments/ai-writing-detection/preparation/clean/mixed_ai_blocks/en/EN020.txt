Not long ago, a friend visited me and noticed my devoted Airedale, affectionately named "Quilp," whose excited tail wagged vigorously at the thought of going for a walk. He casually suggested that with the introduction of new taxes, I might end up paying a guinea just to keep my dog. I expressed my hope that Mr. McKenna, the responsible figure behind these taxes, wouldn't make such an unwise decision. I firmly believe that he will avoid any such folly, as I have always regarded him as a reasonable individual. I argued that while equitable taxation based on income is essential, it is unnecessary for him to dictate how we utilize the money we retain. Why should he strip a less fortunate person of the companionship of a beloved dog, relegating it to the wealthy elite?

Quilp's enthusiastic tail mirrored the intensity of my arguments. He was acutely aware that the discussion revolved around him, his large brown eyes sparkling with understanding, and his expressive brows conveying a mix of self-pity and earnest appeal. He was confident that, regardless of the outcome, he was firmly on my side, ready to defend his honor at a moment’s notice. Yet, being a well-mannered canine, he knew to temper his emotions when in the presence of company.

What impact would such a steep tax have? I implored with fervor. It would undermine the very essence of canine democracy, transforming the entire dog population into a privileged group of spoiled pets. Is there anything more distasteful than the sight of a plump woman in luxurious furs cradling a similarly adorned lap dog? Such a scene is as humiliating for the noble dog lineage as a footman bedecked in gold buttons is for humanity. Ironically, it is precisely these pampered creatures that a high tax would shield. Meanwhile, loyal companions like Quilp—whose tail now danced with pride—dogs that offer unwavering love, run errands, carry burdens, bark protectively, and whose sincerity and loyalty have inspired countless poets, would be taxed out of existence.
