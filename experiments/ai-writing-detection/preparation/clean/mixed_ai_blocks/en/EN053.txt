I want to share with you a remarkable find—a book that deserves to be considered a classic, yet remains largely unknown; a work that few are familiar with outside of my own enthusiasm. Published roughly twelve years ago, it is the most recent offering from a distinguished author. When I mention his name, you’re likely to respond with, “Oh yes! I adore his writings!” and then reference titles like SO-AND-SO along with its equally celebrated continuation, SUCH-AND-SUCH. However, when I inquire if you have come across MY book, you will express astonishment and admit you’ve never heard of it. You might even question, “Is it on par with SO-AND-SO and SUCH-AND-SUCH?” with skepticism in your voice. “Far superior,” I would assert, and if only things were arranged differently, I could pocket an additional ten percent. Still, your appreciation would be enough for me. The author of my highlighted book is none other than Kenneth Grahame. You are familiar with him, right? Excellent, I thought as much. The works you’ve likely encountered are The Golden Age and Dream Days, correct? Thank you for confirming. But the title you’ve yet to read—my book—is The Wind in the Willows. Have I hit the mark again? I feared as much.

The reason I’m confident you haven’t read it is precisely why I refer to it as "my" book. For the past decade or so, I have made it my mission to recommend it. Typically, I bring it up during my initial encounters with new acquaintances. It serves as my opening line, much like how you may discuss the weather. If I don’t manage to mention it right away, I find a way to work it in before the conversation concludes. That stranger is destined to hear about it at some point. Should I ever find myself in a courtroom situation, and one can never be too sure, my response to the judge's inquiry about having any statements would be, “Well, your Honor, may I take a moment to suggest a book to the jury before departing?” Mr. Justice Darling would likely feign familiarity with it, but I would see through that pretense.
