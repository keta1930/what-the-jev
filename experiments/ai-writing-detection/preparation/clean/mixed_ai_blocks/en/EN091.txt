I find solace in patience. I align myself with Wordsworth's notion of "wise passiveness." Attempting to discern my fate from the sole of my boot would be as insightful as interpreting it from the lines in my palm. Both methods would yield the same outcome, revealing only what I wish to see. The same holds true for the celestial bodies. Those who claim to interpret our lives through the movements of the stars are either deluding themselves or misguiding others. They project their own whims onto the cosmos, seeking validation from the universe. A butterfly fluttering in the sunlight outside could just as easily interpret the ongoing European conflict as a reflection of its trivial existence. The stars do not engage in discourse about our lives, yet they offer a soothing presence if we choose to embrace silence. The "huge and thoughtful night" communicates in a manner that is both simple and grand, resonating universally.

One of the minor comforts amidst the war is that it has afforded us in London the opportunity to hear that profound language. With the streetlights extinguished, the celestial lights become more prominent. Almost every weeknight, I board the last northbound bus from the City, and seated at the back on the upper deck, I observe the majestic display of stars. This nightly phenomenon is truly breathtaking. Emerson remarked that if we were to witness it just once every century, we would spend years bracing ourselves for such a sight. Yet, it is available to us every evening, and we often overlook it. Nevertheless, it deserves our attention, serving as a powerful antidote to the frenetic chaos of our lives filled with conflict and strife. The stars provide us with a fresh perspective and a new framework for understanding. In their presence, the ongoing war appears merely a trivial issue within a chaotic institution. I find no need to fret over the vision or demand insights from the stars regarding the conflict; it is sufficient to observe, experience, and remain quiet.

Now, I sincerely hope that Althea will refrain from wasting any more postage on her irreverent musings.
