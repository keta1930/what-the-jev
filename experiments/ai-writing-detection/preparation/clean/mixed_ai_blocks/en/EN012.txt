"Yet, perhaps it won’t resonate with everyone. It’s possible that my ideas are somewhat advanced for their understanding. I recall a particular New Year’s Day when I received an anonymous postcard conveying wishes for a happy New Year. Alongside the greetings, it suggested that I should also extend good wishes to the compositors by being more generous in my writing. At that time, my work averaged a thousand words on a single 8 by 5-inch sheet. I took the card’s advice to heart, though it felt like a struggle, akin to a miniature painter being compelled to depict the Town Council of Boffington in the style of Herkomer for the rest of his career. My canvases may have grown in size since then, yet they remain impressionistic in nature. Observers often comment, “Pretty, but what is it?” They step back and gesture with their hands, saying, “You see it better from here, my love,” as they might express to a spouse. However, if there exists even one compositor who, amidst the frantic pace of life, takes a moment during lunch to appreciate the beautiful words with an artist’s perspective rather than as a mere laborer, I believe he will find joy in my new writing instrument. So, does it truly matter what those who only encounter the printed text think about it?"

"One time, a woman who claimed expertise in the science of calligraphy offered to analyze my personality through my handwriting. I crafted a special sample filled with uplifting phrases like “To be good is to be happy,” “Faith is the lode-star of life,” and “We should always be kind to animals.” I aimed for her to provide an in-depth analysis. After spending the morning on it, she informed me at lunch that I was “synthetic.” You might assume that the compositor had misprinted “synthetic” instead of my intended “sympathetic.” Initially, I too misunderstood her, striving to appear as sympathetic as possible. Yet, she reiterated “synthetic,” leaving no room for confusion. I urged her for further insight, believing each letter would unveil a hidden truth, but all she added was “and not analytic.” For the remainder of the day, I proudly repeated to myself, “I am synthetic! I am synthetic! I am synthetic!” only to follow it up with a tinge of regret, “Alas, I am not analytic!” The meaning of it all eluded me."
