However, these inquiries barely scratch the surface of the enigmas that intrigue me. I am particularly curious about the peculiar and unsettling sounds that emerge during calls. There’s a peculiar tapping noise, reminiscent of a delirious woodpecker, which both astonishes and captivates the listener. How is such a sound produced, and are the creators aware of its impact? Furthermore, why is it that sometimes conversations from other lines can be overheard, while at other times they remain silent? The majority of these snippets are mundane, yet occasionally, they surprise. I hesitate to recount my own lack of insight—a painful reminder of my failure to seize a clear opportunity—on June 4th, 1919, famously known as Derby Day. On that fateful day, the realm of prediction in England reached a low point, as every soothsayer chose The Panther as the winner. During this time, I was subjected to an extended dialogue between what appeared to be a bookmaker and a client regarding a wager on Grand Parade. At first, I found this intrusion bothersome; however, when Grand Parade triumphed at 33 to 1, I later recognized this interruption as a divine nudge I failed to heed. How I lamented my own foolishness!

It is evident that telephony, based on personal experiences and the correspondence I’ve perused, is not yet a precise discipline. In real life, at least; on stage or within the pages of American detective novels, it seems flawless. The actor picks up the receiver, states the number, and begins conversing immediately. If captured on film, their lips move as if they are made of molten rubber, and their mouths become flexible voids. I wonder if the average person mirrors such expressions while on the phone. Perhaps I should set up a mirror to discover the truth.

There are many entertaining anecdotes surrounding telephones. One of my favorites involves a journalist with an exaggerated sense of respect for success, whose boss happens to be a nobleman. Let’s refer to the employer as Lord Forthestait and the journalist as Mr. Blank. While several staff members were engaged in conversation in the newspaper's office, the telephone suddenly rang.

“Mr. Blank, you’re needed on the phone,” called out the clerk.

Blank, who was just about to head out for lunch, returned with irritation and grabbed the receiver. 

“What is it?” he barked.

“Is this Blank?” came the voice on the other end. “Lord Forthestait speaking.”

“Yes, my lord,” replied Blank, instantly adopting a tone of utmost reverence, as he removed his hat.
