I recently embarked on a journey from Keswick, equipped with a rucksack, a Baddeley guide tucked in my pocket, and a friend accompanying me. I appreciate having someone with me during walks. As Sterne noted, "Give me a companion by the way," even if it's just to observe how the shadows stretch as the day fades. That's more than sufficient. A loquacious partner isn't necessary; the act of walking itself is engrossing. While initial conversations can be enjoyable, as you become more immersed in the trek, silence often prevails. You may find yourself falling slightly behind, saving discussions for later at the inn over dinner and a post-supper pipe. A few shared jokes, a snatch of song, or a brief map consultation are more than adequate for the journey.

At the lake's edge, we hopped into a boat and crossed Derwentwater to reach a small cove at the base of Catbells. Once ashore, we hoisted our packs and ventured into the mountains, relishing a week steeped in the profound solitude that this region provides. Instead of adhering to a rigid itinerary, we allowed spontaneity to guide our adventure. While I enjoy crafting walking plans, I find even greater pleasure in deviating from them. One of the true delights of hiking lies in the freedom it affords. You are not bound by schedules, roads, or the expectations of others. You can choose your path; if a particular road appeals to you, then take it. If you prefer a mountain pass, that choice is yours as well. Should your spirit (and stamina) call you to the peaks, then traverse Great Gable, Scawfell, Robinson, or Helvellyn. Every shortcut is an invitation to explore and every trail is a new adventure. The cascading stream down the mountain serves as your refreshing drink. You kneel on the stones, lower your head, and savor the invigorating sips that only the mountains can offer. Then, continuing your trek, you find yourself singing:—

Bed in the bush with the stars to see. Bread I dip in the river— There's the life for a man like me. There's the life for ever.
