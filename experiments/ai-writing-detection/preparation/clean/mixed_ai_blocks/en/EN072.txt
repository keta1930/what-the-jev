I believe we have every reason to voice our grievances. Regardless of our fortunes in other aspects of life, if we lack the fortune associated with inanimate objects, our complaints are valid. While it is undeniably enjoyable to strike it rich with a £500 gain on the Stock Exchange purely by chance, there remains a shadow over this success. The next time we earn £500 through calculated strategy, it will inevitably be attributed to luck as well. It is curious how luck is credited with our achievements while the other individual is seen as merely fortunate. This disparity is precisely why we can justifiably express our dissatisfaction.

It puzzles me why circumstances often seem to conspire against certain individuals. Perhaps there’s a form of justice at play. It’s conceivable—if not likely—that those favored by good fortune may be disliked by animals, children, and even their peers. I, for one, harbor a dislike for such characters. The more I contemplate their nature, the clearer it becomes that they possess few redeeming qualities. They are, in essence, unremarkable. It is hardly surprising that they receive minimal compensation in life, such as an inexplicable ability to thrive with inanimate objects. This ability, often mistaken for skill and quickness, can mislead the less discerning. Should you encounter someone renowned for such traits, you will likely perceive their true character.

Due to the peculiar circumstances surrounding my morning routine, I find myself composing this article while clad in a dressing gown. Now, I will don a collar and hope it produces a pleasant squeak.
