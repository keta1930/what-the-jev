"The veins exhibited dilation and thickening, characterized by elongation and twisting, along with protruding areas reminiscent of knots or knobs. Within these formations, blood tends to coagulate, leading to fibrin accumulation, and occasionally, even bone-like matter appears at the center. Furthermore, the vein walls themselves are affected by disease."

However, the situation was far more complex than it initially seemed. It was undoubtedly a serious condition. Numerous individuals had been rejected due to varicose veins, and the medical professionals needed to be informed about the severity of the issue. This was imperative. He believed he should not enter the military under false pretenses. While he fervently desired to enlist and contribute to the war effort—more than many others—he questioned whether it was just to withhold the truth from the doctors regarding his condition. He was acutely aware of the knots and knobs that manifested when he exerted himself. Who could say? Perhaps there was indeed "fibrin" and "osseous matter" present. Regardless, it was essential for the doctors to evaluate his leg under proper circumstances.

He was firmly against allowing his sense of patriotism to lead him into deceiving his country. It would not be right for the nation to invest significant resources in an individual who might become incapacitated within a short time. This was not just unfair to the country; it was also unjust to himself. Nevertheless, his thoughts were not solely focused on his own well-being. He was considering the interests of his country. At times, one must prioritize the nation's needs above personal feelings. He recognized his responsibility to be transparent about his condition. He longed to participate in the war as much as anyone but was unwilling to be a liability to his country.

Indeed, he believed it was his obligation to walk openly with this condition. He felt it was necessary to disclose the presence of those knots and knobs on his leg. He hoped they would not pose an insurmountable barrier to his enlistment. Regardless of the outcome, he was resolved to be honest with his country. He would demonstrate to the doctor exactly what his varicose veins entailed.
