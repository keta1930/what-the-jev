The joy of childhood that remains irretrievable is the delight of splashing in the ocean. Near Fleet Street stands a statue honoring the individual who pioneered Sunday schools in England, but the true hero deserving of a lasting tribute in stone is the doctor who boldly claimed that saltwater could not cause a cold. Whether this assertion held any truth is uncertain, but it served as an excellent response to the worries of concerned adults and significantly enhanced the pleasures of a day at the beach. Unfortunately, this joy has faded for those of us who have grown up and taken charge of our own lives. Take me, for instance; while I can choose to wade into freshwater whenever I please, dipping my toes into saltwater no longer feels like a special treat.

Given my feelings and the reflections I have shared, it saddens me to realize that if I were to visit the coast this August, it wouldn't be with a sandcastle bucket and spade but rather with a set of golf clubs. My evenings would not be spent lounging on the shore but instead cycling to the nearest town to pick up a newspaper. However, it would be pointless to assert that my love for the sea has diminished or that I have outgrown my fondness for those childhood joys. I firmly believe that it is the sea itself that has changed over time, while I find contentment in my thoughts of it as it once was, nestled in my memories while I remain in Fleet Street.
