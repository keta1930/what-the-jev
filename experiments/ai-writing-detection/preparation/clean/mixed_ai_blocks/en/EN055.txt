"Occasionally, I have shared in this column stories of real events that have transpired. I do not suggest that my column is unique in this regard among the myriad of newspaper columns. Rather, I have found that certain practical parables drawn from everyday experiences convey my thoughts more effectively than other means. Thus, I wish to recount the encounter with an extraordinary cab driver that took place just three days ago. Despite its seemingly trivial nature, this incident stirred within me a fleeting moment of profound emotion, teetering on despair.

On the day of my encounter with the unusual cab driver, I had just enjoyed lunch at a quaint little restaurant in Soho with a few close friends. My companions are a mix of relentless skeptics and fervent believers, which inevitably led our lunchtime discussion to revolve around some of life’s most significant and daunting concepts. Ultimately, our debate circled back to one central theme: can a person truly be certain of anything? I contend that certainty is indeed possible, as I expressed to my friend while passionately waving an empty bottle. If one cannot intellectually conceive of certainty, how can we even label something as uncertain? For instance, if I have never encountered certainty, I cannot assert that something lacks it. Likewise, if I have no experience of the color green, how can I claim my nose isn’t green? It could very well be vibrantly green for all I know, given my lack of exposure to the concept. Our voices raised in spirited disagreement, the atmosphere grew charged, for metaphysics is inherently emotional. The divide between us ran deep, rooted in our differing views on the essence of broad-mindedness or intellectual openness. My friend compared his approach to the sun unfurling the fronds of a palm tree, opening endlessly for the sake of mere openness. In contrast, I likened my intellect's expansion to opening my mouth, only to subsequently close it around something substantial. I was, in fact, engaged in this very act at that moment. It would indeed appear rather absurd if I continued to open my mouth infinitely without purpose."
