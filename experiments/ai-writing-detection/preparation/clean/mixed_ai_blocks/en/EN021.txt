In my view, a country stick must have the versatility to be grasped from either end and used for striking a ball. Therefore, it should lack a ferrule, featuring a handle that is both heavy and straight. This requirement reflects the origins of golf, which emerged when its inventor wandered through fields post-picnic, playfully hitting a cork from his bottle. Initially, he recorded a score of seventy-nine strokes from one field's gate to an oak tree in another, later improving to fifty-four. It was at this moment that he truly recognized the essence of the game. Clearly, he cherished Nature, as the impulse to engage in playful activities with a stick is often strongest when one is in high spirits, likely inspired by the beauty of streams, hedges, and sunlight streaming through the trees. Thus, my ideal stick should possess a heavy, straight handle, devoid of a ferrule, and I must ensure that I carry an old golf ball with me.

Conversely, in London, we tend to be less discerning about our sticks. Primarily, we need a support for leaning against while conversing with a suddenly encountered friend. After the customary greeting of "Hulloa!" and the realization that we have little else of substance to discuss, the presence of our stick significantly alleviates the awkwardness. It provides both moral and physical support, akin to the comfort offered by a cigarette in a drawing-room setting. For this urban purpose, the size and shape of the stick are not particularly critical. However, it is crucial that it is not overly slippery, as nervousness could cause us to drop it entirely.
