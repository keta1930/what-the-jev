I now find myself regretting that this theory has been introduced into public discourse. Indeed, I hold myself accountable for amplifying its presence. In the past, when we acquired— or, more accurately, received—a cigar as a gift, our only concern was whether it was of high quality. We would simply bite one end and ignite the other, and once our uncertainty was resolved, we could relax and savor the experience. However, this tranquility is about to be disrupted. We will become painfully self-aware. Every time we remove our cigars, we’ll sense the scrutiny of those around us, all while struggling to recall which of the numerous ways to hold a cigar signifies the utmost virtue. Personally, I tend to grip my cigar in a variety of positions throughout the evening (though, of course, never with the end resting on a knife), and I shudder at the complex judgments that the modern Wellingtons at the table might make about me. It appears that I must now focus on adopting a single, consistent method. If only I could remember which one presents me in the best light!

Yet, the tobacco test is merely one of several indicators. Our manner of clasping our hands or the angle of a walking stick could expose our true selves as well. It is futile to emulate the archetype of the strong, reserved man from literature, whose expression serves as a barrier to his feelings. This realization is regrettable; I have come to believe that it is indeed unfortunate. If I have a hidden vice, such as forging checks, I certainly do not wish it to be broadcasted through the tilt of my hat; even less do I want to uncover such a flaw in a friend I cherish or one whom I can easily outplay at billiards.

Imagine how monotonous life would be if we could instantly discern every detail about our acquaintances the moment we offered them our cigar case. Consider this extreme scenario: what if a charming bachelor, who admires our bowling skills, revealed his true nature as a secret abuser through the way his shoelaces are tied? What could we possibly do? Would we sever ties with such a unique individual? Absolutely not. Let us hope to remain blissfully unaware of the imperfections of those we hold dear, just as we fervently wish for them to remain in the dark about our own shortcomings.
