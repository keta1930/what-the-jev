This example illustrates that whether a habit stems from a mental frailty or a physical flaw, it can lead to highly embarrassing situations, much like the banker who faced ruin after unintentionally disclosing sensitive information while out on the street. How can one maintain confidentiality or negotiate effectively if one’s tongue is uncontrollable? For instance, what value does it hold for Jones to explain to his wife that he was delayed at work when his tongue inadvertently reveals, without his awareness, that had he declared “no trumps” during that last card game, he would have finished the night up at the club? Such scenarios evoke distressing images of domestic strife and public calamity arising from this rather common trait.

Conversely, could there also be advantages in a society where expressing our thoughts verbally was the norm? Envision a reality where everyone was devoid of secrets—where secrets were impossible. I can picture the Kaiser, who claims his sole aim is peace, unconsciously divulging to the British Ambassador that the ultimatum directed at Serbia is merely a ruse—indicating that Germany’s true intention is war and that an invasion of Belgium is imminent. I imagine the British Ambassador, having conveyed that England has no obligations, remarking dreamily, “But if war occurs, we shall be involved.” Similarly, after making a solid offer of £30 for Smith's horse, Jones might absentmindedly add, “Naturally, it would be a bargain at £50, and I could stretch to £55 if he pushes back on the price.”

In such a world, lies would lose their significance, and deception would become an exercise in futility—truth would no longer languish at the bottom of the well but would instead be readily available at the tip of everyone’s tongue. All the con artists would find themselves incarcerated, and dishonest merchants would face bankruptcy proceedings. The practice of secret diplomacy, which often jeopardizes lives, would cease to exist, as there would be no secrets left to keep. Those little, nagging secrets that can devastate lives would be eradicated. You, sir, who find it easy to criticize at home yet struggle to utter the kind words you know to be true, would discover this tendency to your benefit, leading to a more harmonious household.

Indeed, I believe the world could function quite well if our tongues naturally expressed our genuine thoughts, regardless of our intentions. However, the number of individuals who would be exposed is considerable. Just the notion makes my face flush, and I suspect yours does as well.
