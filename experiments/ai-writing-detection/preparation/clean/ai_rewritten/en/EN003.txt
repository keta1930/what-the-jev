In a world filled with countless marriages and decisive battles, it appears that much can be achieved. We, at certain moments of the day, eagerly and purposefully pack away our sustenance into the bag that represents our existence. On the surface, it might seem that the pursuit of acquiring as much as possible is the primary objective of humanity's often contentious journey. However, this perception is a mere illusion when it comes to the essence of our spirit. Living happily places us on an ascending scale, where one experience naturally flows into another in an infinite sequence. There is always a new horizon for those who aspire, and although we inhabit a small planet, engaged in trivial matters and fleeting moments of life, our hopes remain as distant as the stars. The act of hoping can stretch out for the entirety of our lives. True happiness hinges on our beginnings rather than our conclusions, on our desires instead of our possessions. Aspirations provide a perpetual joy, akin to the ownership of real estate—an inexhaustible fortune that yields a continuous stream of enjoyable activities. The presence of numerous aspirations renders a person spiritually affluent. Without such interests, life becomes a dull and poorly directed performance; for those lacking art or science, the world is reduced to a mere collage of colors or a perilous path fraught with potential missteps. 

It is through our own desires and curiosities that we endure life with any semblance of patience. These elements enchant our perception of the world, enabling us to see its beauty and complexity. Each day we awaken with a revived enthusiasm for work and pleasure, driven by desire and curiosity, which serve as the lenses through which we view the world in vibrant hues. They transform mundane experiences into captivating ones, whether it’s the allure of a person or the intrigue of a fossil. A person may lose their wealth and fall into destitution, yet if they retain these two treasures, they still possess the potential for joy. Imagine a scenario where one could consume a single, all-encompassing meal that eliminates hunger forever, or to absorb all knowledge in an instant—wouldn’t such an existence lead to a lack of amusement thereafter?

Consider a traveler who walks with only a single book in their knapsack. This individual reads attentively, frequently pausing to think deeply or to observe the landscape around them, reluctant to deplete their source of entertainment and find themselves alone on the remaining stretch of their journey. A young man recently completed his reading of Thomas Carlyle's works, concluding with the ten notebooks on Frederick the Great. "What!" he exclaimed in dismay, "Is there no more Carlyle? Am I to be left with nothing but the daily papers?" A well-known instance involves Alexander the Great, who wept for the absence of new worlds to conquer. Similarly, Gibbon felt a profound melancholy upon finishing the "Decline and Fall," experiencing only fleeting joy before parting from his monumental work.

Fortunately, we all aim for the moon with our ineffective attempts. Our aspirations reach for elusive dreams, and we never truly conclude our endeavors here on earth. Interests seem to sprout only to be replanted, much like mustard seeds. One might think that the arrival of a child would signify the end of worries, yet it merely signifies the dawn of new anxieties. Once one has navigated through the child’s teething, education, and ultimately marriage, fresh fears and heightened sensitivities arise daily. The health of grandchildren becomes as pressing a concern as one’s own. After marrying, one might believe they have reached a peak and can begin a gentle descent. However, the end of courting simply leads into the complexities of marriage. While the pursuit of love can be arduous for obstinate spirits, maintaining that love is equally demanding, requiring both partners to invest kindness and goodwill. The true narrative of love begins at the altar, where the newlyweds face a beautiful contest of wisdom and generosity, along with a lifelong pursuit toward an ideal that remains perpetually out of reach. Is it unattainable? Indeed, it is, simply because there are two individuals instead of one.

The Preacher lamented that there is no end to the creation of books, perhaps unaware of the high regard in which he inadvertently held literature as an endeavor. There truly is no limit to the production of books, experiments, travels, or the accumulation of wealth. Each problem generates further inquiries. Our studies can continue indefinitely, yet we never attain the level of knowledge we desire. We have yet to create a statue that reflects our aspirations. Even when we discover a new continent or traverse a mountain range, we inevitably encounter another ocean or plain waiting on the other side. The universe is boundless, offering ample space for our most diligent pursuits and more. Unlike the works of Carlyle, which can be read to completion, even the smallest corner of the world presents ever-changing weather and seasons, ensuring that a lifetime spent there reveals constant surprises and delights.

Ultimately, there exists only one desire that can be fully realized on this earth; just one thing can be attained without reservation: death. Yet, due to various circumstances, we lack anyone to inform us whether this outcome is worth pursuing.

As we journey toward our elusive dreams, we paint a peculiar picture, marching onward while begrudging ourselves the necessary moments of rest; we are tireless and adventurous pioneers. It is true that we may never reach our goals; it is highly likely that no such destinations exist. Even if we lived for centuries and possessed divine powers, we would find ourselves not significantly closer to our desires at the end. Oh, the laboring hands of humanity! Oh, the tireless feet of those wandering without knowing their destination! It often feels as though we are on the brink of reaching a significant high point, just a little further until we can spot the towers of El Dorado against the setting sun. Little do we understand our own blessedness, for to travel with hope is far more rewarding than simply arriving, and true success lies in the act of striving itself.
