The invitation card, which I must admit I had somewhat eagerly anticipated, finally arrived, specifying that my attendance was requested the following day at noon sharp, with a dress code of evening attire. Since I received this notice late into the night, I had a flurry of tasks to complete the next morning. I needed to procure medals for distribution among certain acquaintances, and I arranged for a carriage and pair to take my friend and myself to the reception. Our intention was to make a grand entrance through the prestigious gate of honor, which required a two-horse carriage; otherwise, we would find ourselves deposited at a lesser door, followed by a lengthy walk.

We were on our way to one of the most renowned buildings in the world—perhaps the most celebrated of all. The sound of the horses’ hooves echoed boldly against the cobblestones, a sound that evoked images reminiscent of Dumas as we moved swiftly under grand archways, past sentinels, and through expansive, historic courtyards until we reached the base of the iconic stairway. After we ascended to an ante-room, we were scrutinized by impressive guards while splendid attendants relieved us of our hats. We were then ushered into the reception room, where an elderly gentleman in black, holding a black bag, was engaged in an animated conversation with a major-domo, forcing us to interrupt their discussion to make our entrance.

Inside this opulent reception room, where we were to meet our host, an adequate number of guests had already gathered to nearly fill the available wall space. We were arranged in four lines, positioned with our backs against the walls. I estimated the total number of attendees to be around ninety, among whom were numerous priests and nuns, a good number of women, some young men, a handful of girls, and very few civilian men. A quick glance made it evident that my friend and I were the only ones adhering to the evening dress requirement. This distinction, of course, heightened our sense of comfort, rendering us the focal point, or the "cynosure," of the gathering, as one might say.

In the center of the room, a small group of officials, including several soldiers, engaged in quiet conversation while occasionally casting glances towards the opposite door leading to a long corridor. We waited nervously for about twenty minutes, whispering amongst ourselves until, without warning, the officials straightened, the soldiers hurriedly retrieved their rifles from a distant corner—an action that struck me as somewhat amusing given the circumstances—and we all sank to our knees as a brisk, dark man dressed in white entered the room.

Kneeling in evening attire at twelve twenty in the afternoon, facing an array of others similarly positioned across an expansive carpet, while being somewhat self-conscious and hungry, is hardly conducive to careful observation. Yet, I managed to note that our host's movements were swift and bird-like, accompanied by a keen, penetrating, and rapid gaze. Beneath his cassock, the flash of elaborate slippers caught my eye, complemented by a large and stunning emerald ring that adorned his hand.

Due to his tardiness, he wasted no time and got straight to the matter at hand. Each visitor was to be acknowledged individually. Some had arrived with particular dilemmas requiring guidance, while others sought comfort for their absent loved ones; many, like me, had medals that needed to be imbued with greater efficacy. Additionally, a few priests had brought along elderly parishioners who appreciated the significance of such an event and would cherish the opportunity for a bit of dialogue. The host had much to accomplish, yet he approached his duties with evident tact and consideration. When he finally reached me, he offered no words, but my friend—a man of the old tradition—posed a question regarding a dying youth and received enough reassurance to find solace. Throughout this, I noticed the elderly gentleman in black, with his black bag, glancing around from the doorway—his role, as I later learned, was that of a physician ready to revive those who often succumb to the emotional weight of the occasion.

Once he had addressed the final visitor, our host moved to the center of the room and delivered a brief address about the significance of his blessing and the value of maintaining moral integrity. After this, he blessed us collectively one last time before taking his leave. We struggled to rise, the elderly ladies finding their priests’ assistance invaluable in this endeavor.

My knees were quite sore from kneeling, but in the grand scheme of things, such discomfort mattered little to me. I had just witnessed Pope Benedict XV in person.
