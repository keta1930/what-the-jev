During a recent dinner at a popular restaurant, I couldn't help but notice a man seated alone at a nearby table who appeared to be deeply engrossed in a conversation with himself. His face displayed a range of emotions, predominantly intense anger, as he animatedly spoke, only pausing occasionally to chew his food. Several diners cast furtive glances in his direction, yet he remained oblivious to their attention, seemingly unaware he was acting unusually. When he finally stood to leave, his earlier agitation seemed to have dissipated, and he resembled a man who had successfully argued his case.

This behavior of talking to oneself likely harbors a less ominous implication than it may first appear. It could simply stem from a surge of mental energy and an intense focus that completely excludes the outside world. In the incident I observed, it was apparent that the man had mentally detached from his surroundings, absorbed in his thoughts to the point where he neither saw nor heard anything around him. He was lost in contemplation, perhaps even engaged in a debate with an imagined opponent, and only returned to reality when he completed his meal and settled his bill. He seemed to have transitioned from a different state of awareness, akin to waking from a vivid, chaotic dream. It’s clear he had no idea he was vocally expressing his thoughts for a good half-hour, and I suspect that if someone had pointed this out to him, he would have fervently denied it, much like anyone would contest the notion of snoring while asleep. He simply wasn’t aware.

A troubling thought then crossed my mind. What if I, too, engage in self-dialogue? What if, like this man, I become so engrossed in my internal narrative that I cannot hear my own voice chattering away? It’s a disconcerting notion. Even a strong belief that I don’t talk to myself is insufficient reassurance. This man likely held a firm belief against it—he might have even found humor in the idea of someone conversing with themselves as he walked by. The fact that my friends have never mentioned this particular quirk provides little comfort. They might think I enjoy talking to myself or, perhaps more likely, they understand that I’m not keen on hearing about my flaws. I feel the need to monitor my behavior, but then again, how can one consciously oversee the unconscious? It’s as futile as attempting to control one’s dreams. If I’m unaware that I’m speaking, how can I possibly stop?

Then, a comforting realization struck me. I could recall instances where I had indeed spoken to myself, and I was fully aware of my voice. These moments usually occurred while playing golf, where I’d mutter emphatic remarks about the unpredictability of golf clubs or the obstinacy of golf balls. In those instances, the sound of my own voice startled me back to reality, prompting a quick glance to see if the woman in the red jacket playing nearby had overheard me, or worse, had seen my outburst.

This serves as clear evidence, I believe. A habitual self-talker is often oblivious to their own utterances. Their spoken words are merely echoes of their thoughts, so closely aligned that, much like harmonious musical chords, there is no discord. A similar scenario unfolded when I observed an art student meticulously replicating a painting at the Tate Gallery. He would comment aloud to himself, saying, “Ah, a touch more blue,” as he shifted his gaze between the original artwork and his canvas. A moment later, he remarked, “Yes, that line needs better drawing.” Those standing nearby chuckled at his candid thoughts, yet he appeared completely unaware of his vocalizations.

There are certainly cases where the conscious and unconscious mind seem to intertwine, where intentional statements slip out alongside unintentional ones. A notable example is Thomas Landseer, father of the renowned Sir Edwin. On one occasion, while visiting a fellow artist and admiring their work, he exclaimed, “Ah, very nice indeed! Excellent color; excellent!” Then, as if lost in his own world, he added, “Poor chap, he thinks he can paint!” 

This incident illustrates that whether this tendency results from a mental flaw or a simple physical quirk, it can lead to awkward situations—as evidenced by the banker who found himself financially ruined after inadvertently divulging confidential information while strolling down the street. How can one maintain a secret or negotiate a deal when their tongue acts without restraint? What good does it do for Jones to explain to his wife that he was held up at work if his tongue betrays him by revealing that he could have pocketed a considerable amount had he declared “no trumps” in that last hand? I envision disastrous domestic entanglements and public calamities stemming from this not-so-rare habit.

Yet, could there be a benefit to a collective practice of voicing our thoughts aloud? Imagine a society where no one could harbor secrets from anyone else. Picture a scenario where the Kaiser, after solemnly declaring his commitment to peace, unwittingly confides to the British Ambassador that the ultimatum to Serbia is merely a ruse—that Germany’s true intent is war and plans to invade Belgium. Simultaneously, the British Ambassador, while stating that England has no obligations, absentmindedly adds, “But should there be a war, we will certainly be involved.”

In such a world, deception would lose its value, and dishonesty would become an ineffective strategy. Truth would no longer be buried at the bottom of a well but would instead be readily available at the forefront of everyone’s speech. We would see con artists behind bars and fraudulent merchants facing bankruptcy. Secretive diplomacy would no longer jeopardize lives, as no secrets would exist. Those little deceitful cover-ups that ruin so many lives would vanish. You, sir, who find it effortless to nag at home yet struggle to express the kind words you know to be true, would be revealed to your great advantage, ultimately bringing peace to your household.

Indeed, I believe the world could thrive in an environment where our tongues articulated our honest thoughts, regardless of our intentions. However, the implications of such transparency are daunting. The thought of being exposed fills me with embarrassment—and I imagine it does the same for you.
