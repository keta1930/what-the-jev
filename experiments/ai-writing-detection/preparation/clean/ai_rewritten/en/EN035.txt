During my childhood, I encountered an individual who epitomized the men who shaped the Empire—a figure clad in an astracan coat, sporting a matching astracan moustache, which was tight, black, and curly. It remains unclear whether he donned the moustache to complement the coat or if his formidable will allowed him not just to grow facial hair in the conventional sense but also to sprout smaller moustaches across his garments. I distinctly remember him telling me, “A man can’t get on nowadays by hanging about with his hands in his pockets.” To this, I flippantly retorted that perhaps success came from having one’s hands in others’ pockets. This prompted him to delve into a discussion on Moral Evolution, suggesting that my quip held some grain of truth. This recollection now links to a more recent experience that I find relevant.

The one time I attempted pickpocketing, I inadvertently ended up robbing myself. It was an unintentional act, but it certainly bore the emotional weight of a thief's endeavor. As I rummaged through my own pockets, I was gripped by a blend of anxiety and curiosity, wondering what treasures I might uncover. I wouldn’t necessarily call myself a neat individual, but I can generally account for all my belongings. I always know their whereabouts and what I’ve done with them—unless they find their way into my pockets. Once something slips into those mysterious depths, it feels like a somber farewell to it, akin to a Virgilian goodbye. I assume the items I’ve lost in my pockets remain there, just as those submerged in the sea are presumed to be lost forever. I regard the contents of both these vast voids with a blend of reverence and ignorance. It is said that the sea will reveal its hidden treasures on the last day, and I imagine that would also apply to the unusual items lurking in my pockets. Yet, I cannot recall what any of them are, save for the money; finding anything else would come as a complete surprise.

This encapsulates my previously untainted state of innocence. I wish to briefly share the specific, remarkable, and previously unmatched circumstances that drove me, in a moment of clarity and sound mind, to empty my pockets. I found myself confined in a third-class train carriage for an extended journey. It was nearing evening, though time felt irrelevant as the relentless rain cloaked the world outside, blurring the lines between earth, sky, light, and shadow. Without a book, newspaper, or even a scrap of paper to jot down thoughts, I was left in a state of utter boredom. There were no advertisements to peruse; otherwise, I could have delved into the depths of their printed words, as even the simplest phrase could ignite a cascade of mental exploration. Encountering a phrase like “Sunlight Soap” could lead me to ponder themes of sun worship, the mythology of Apollo, or the poetry of summer before reluctantly transitioning to the mundane topic of soap. However, inside that carriage, I was surrounded by unadorned wood, both within and outside, devoid of any distractions. I firmly assert that nothing is inherently uninteresting, so I began to scrutinize the joints of the walls and seats, contemplating the intriguing nature of wood. Just as I started to reflect on the significance of Christ being a carpenter rather than pursuing other trades, I suddenly jolted upright, remembering my pockets. I realized I was carrying an unknown trove of items—an eclectic collection akin to the British Museum or the South Kensington collections, all tucked away on my person. It was time to investigate.

The first discovery I unearthed was a mountain of Battersea tram tickets. They poured out like confetti, enough to host an extravagant paper chase. These tickets evoked a wave of patriotic sentiment and even brought tears to my eyes. More importantly, they served as the printed material I craved, as I found brief yet compelling scientific essays about some kind of pill printed on their backs. In my state of destitution, those tickets could be considered a modest yet well-curated scientific library. If my train journey were to persist for a few more months, I could foresee myself deeply engaging in the debates surrounding the pill, crafting responses and counter-arguments based on the data at hand. However, it was the symbolic essence of those tickets that truly resonated with me. Just as the cross of St. George signifies English patriotism, those scraps represented the burgeoning municipal pride that currently stands as one of England’s greatest hopes.

Next, I extracted a pocketknife from my collection. A pocketknife, as one can imagine, could warrant an entire volume filled with moral reflections. It embodies one of the most fundamental practical origins upon which civilization is built, resting on the solid foundations of human ingenuity. The very essence of metals—especially iron and steel—pulled me into a reverie. I envisioned the primal reality where the first human, standing among ordinary stones, discovered something extraordinary. I imagined a fierce struggle where stone axes shattered and stone knives splintered against something gleaming and novel in the hands of a determined person. I could almost hear the clanging of hammers on anvils worldwide, and I visualized the swords of feudal conflicts and the scars of industrial warfare. A knife is merely a diminutive sword, and a pocketknife can be seen as a concealed sword. As I opened it, gazing at the sharp, glinting blade, I pondered whether it symbolizes one of humanity's oldest needs. Just as I formed this thought, I was surprised to find the next item emerging from my pocket was a box of matches. In that moment, I recognized fire—an element more potent than steel, an ancient and fierce force that we cherish yet approach with caution.

Continuing my exploration, I stumbled upon a piece of chalk, which evoked a vision of all the art and frescoes that have adorned our world. Following that was a modest coin, which not only bore the likeness and inscription of our own Caesar but also represented the very essence of governance and societal order throughout history. However, this is merely a glimpse of the myriad poetic symbols that cascaded from my pockets; I cannot possibly recount everything that emerged. There is one notable absence from my collection, though, that I must mention—my railway ticket.
