Wellington is reputed to have selected his officers based on the shapes of their noses and chins. Judging by the Duke’s own portraits, one might infer that his standards for noses were quite high, although he likely made exceptions. Regardless, this peculiar method allowed him to assemble the men he deemed suitable. Some might argue that it would have been wiser for him to consider the mouth as the more telling feature. After all, a person's nose is largely predetermined at birth; a baby born with a flat nose might find it rather unfortunate to have their potential deemed unworthy for Wellington's ranks so early in life. Conversely, a child with a more prominent nose could just as easily suffer an accident that would alter it, thereby jeopardizing any future military aspirations. This seems rather unfair, wouldn’t you agree?

In contrast, the mouth offers a far more reliable reflection of one's character. A person can clench their teeth, smile dismissively, or sneer, with all these expressions manifesting more through the mouth than the nose or chin. Emotions are primarily conveyed through the mouth and eyes, and thus one would expect these areas to bear the traces of our feelings. While I once came across a story of a man whose nose quivered with rage, such occurrences are rare; I have yet to encounter anyone whose chin exhibited any notable behavior. It would be quite absurd to anticipate such a thing.

However, a challenge arises when we consider the possibility that a person might disguise their true nature with a moustache, effectively concealing their mouth. Furthermore, someone who appears foolish with their mouth perpetually agape might simply be suffering from allergies. The complications involved in accurately gauging someone's character based solely on their facial features seem to multiply by the minute. So, how do we determine whether we can trust someone with our most cherished possessions, be it our daughter or our favorite golf club?

Thankfully, a timely article on the "cigar manner" has provided some insights. The author suggests that one can discern a man's character through the way he handles his cigar, offering a dozen examples to support this theory. Admittedly, this perspective overlooks those who don’t smoke cigars; one might be inclined to categorize non-smokers as rather foolish in a tongue-in-cheek manner. Nonetheless, for those who do smoke, this theory purportedly reveals significant insights into their personalities—essentially focusing on the individuals who truly matter.

I won’t divulge all the specifics of this theory just yet; for one, I risk infringing on someone else's intellectual property, and secondly, I’ve unfortunately forgotten many of the details. However, the general idea suggests that a man who grips his cigar between his thumb and finger is characterized as brave and compassionate towards animals, while one who holds it between his first and second fingers is deemed impulsive yet kind to the elderly. If he chooses to hold it upside down, he is labeled as both foolish and self-centered, and if he uses a knife to smoke it down to the very end, well, that’s a clear indication of meanness. One can see how a simple cigar might provide such useful insights.

Reflecting on this theory, I find myself regretting its introduction to the public sphere. Yes, I take some responsibility for giving it more attention. In days gone by, when we received a cigar as a gift or purchased one, our only concern would be whether it was of good quality. We would merely bite one end, light the other, and proceed to enjoy it without a second thought. Now, however, everything is set to change. We will become painfully self-aware, acutely aware of the gazes from those around us as we attempt to recall the specific way to hold our cigars that signifies the pinnacle of virtue. Personally, I find myself switching between various grips throughout an evening (though never, of course, on the end of a knife), and I shudder at the complex persona that the modern-day Wellingtons of the table might ascribe to me. Moving forward, I see the necessity of settling on a single method. If only I could remember which one portrays my best self!

But the cigar test isn’t the sole measure available to us. Our hand-closing techniques and the angle at which we carry a walking stick may also reveal our true selves. It’s futile to emulate the archetypal strong, silent man from novels, whose expression serves as a barrier to his emotions. I find this rather unfortunate. If I have a hidden flaw, such as cheque forgery, I would prefer it remain concealed rather than exposed by the tilt of my hat. Even less would I wish to uncover such faults in a friend, especially someone I appreciate or can outplay at billiards.

Imagine how mundane life would become if we could instantly understand every acquaintance's character simply by offering them a cigar. Take an extreme example: what if a charming young bachelor, who admires our bowling prowess, revealed himself through his shoelaces to be a secret wife-beater? What recourse would we have? Would we sever ties with such a unique individual? Certainly not. Let us hope to remain blissfully unaware of the flaws of those we care about, just as we wish for them to remain ignorant of our own shortcomings.
