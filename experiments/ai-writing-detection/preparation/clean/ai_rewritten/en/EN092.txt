The recent introduction of the wasp gun has generated excitement in various circles. Conceptually, it resembles a letter clip; you simply squeeze the trigger, and the upper and lower plates snap together with a speed that would undoubtedly catch any unfortunate insect caught in between off guard. The challenge, however, lies in positioning the insect accurately before taking aim. I envision a great deal of enjoyment stemming from a wasp-hunting expedition. Picture us stationed at the edge of the marmalade, while the beaters work through the spread; with enough wasp guns on hand, few insects will manage to escape. Of course, having a loader to clean the weapon periodically will be essential to maintain its effectiveness.

Nonetheless, I harbor concerns that society may frown upon the wasp gun. Whenever something appears both practical and convenient, it often faces disdain from the more discerning members of society. I can easily envision a pompous individual being labeled as “the type who would opt for a wasp gun over a teaspoon.” It is a known fact that hat-guards are seen as the hallmark of a rather inferior character. The underlying belief seems to be that individuals like you and me, being somewhat affluent, are indifferent to the loss of our straw hats, which could easily blow away into the Serpentine. It is only the unfortunate clerk, who can’t afford a new hat each day, that must take precautions to safeguard his only one. Yet, how practical and efficient is the hat-guard! One can only imagine the pride its inventor felt upon creating it. He likely envisioned a statue erected in his honor on Cromwell Road, a fitting tribute for someone who contributed to public welfare. It’s unfortunate he didn’t grasp that the utility of his creation likely deemed it socially unacceptable.

Take, for instance, the dicky or "Richard." What could be more stylish or functional? Yet, we tend to avoid wearing one. I recall a striking predicament recounted in a story by W. S. Jackson. The protagonist found himself stranded at a foreign hotel without his belongings. To his surprise, the girl he secretly adored arrived at the hotel with her father. He received an invitation to dine with them, but in a pinch, he had to borrow clothes from accommodating waiters, one of which included a dicky. The dinner commenced splendidly, and our hero made a good impression, with the atmosphere filled with joy. However, chaos erupted when a candle was accidentally knocked over, igniting the heroine's dress. 

In that moment, the hero instinctively understood the need to act, recalling how heroes traditionally remove their coats to wrap around burning damsels. He sprang to his feet (or whatever moves the fastest) — and then he remembered. He was wearing a dicky! Without his coat, he would reveal his faux pas to the very person he wished to impress the most. But if he kept his coat on, she might perish. It was a truly dreadful dilemma. I’ve forgotten how he resolved it, but I suspect you and I would have safeguarded the secret of the dicky at any cost. Ultimately, what is inherently wrong with a false shirt-front? The only issue it poses is the impression of the wearer’s financial situation. We, who have the luxury of buying new straw hats daily, are unconcerned about laundry expenses; yet, how embarrassing it would be if anyone suspected we were worried about them.

When it comes to attire, a gentleman often only learns about the taboos of dressing. It would be intriguing if women could enlighten us on what behaviors are deemed unacceptable for a true lady. I once heard a woman disparaged because she styled her hair with just two bobby pins, highlighting that poor feminine etiquette can be found in many areas. Yet, it seems the appearance of financial struggle, whether from lack of means or leisure, is the chief offense that must be avoided at all costs.

So, why should the wasp gun be deemed unacceptable? I cannot pinpoint it, but I have a strong intuition that it will be. Perhaps the wasp gun suggests a deficiency in silver spoons designed for such lethal purposes. Maybe it reflects an excessive regard for the marmalade itself. A wealthy individual would simply drown the wasp in the jar with their spoon and casually request another pot to be opened. In contrast, a less fortunate person waits on the sidelines with their gun, while the marmalade remains free of casualties, ready to be served. The gun, in essence, broadcasts your financial status; thus, it is best to be avoided.

Despite these concerns, I find myself wanting one. I have managed to steer clear of hat-guards, dickies, and prefabricated ties without fully understanding why, but truly, I haven’t felt the absence of them. The wasp gun, however, is a different story; having laid eyes on it, I sense I would be discontent without it. Wasp hunting promises to be a thrilling activity; it will require a steady hand, sharp eyesight, and a measure of bravery. When the season commences, I will be there, regardless of whether it is considered acceptable or not. Our first target will be the apple-quince coverts. “Hornet over!”
