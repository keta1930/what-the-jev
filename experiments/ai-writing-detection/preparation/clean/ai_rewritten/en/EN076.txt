The joyful man attracts cheerful faces around him; a sorrowful expression serves as a reminder of his bliss. So, as I board the 2:10 train on Saturday, I generously share my good fortune. The cab driver, sensing that he needs to engage with me, remarks that I am the first gentleman he has encountered that day. He sees through my casual attire and refers to me as "captain," leaving ambiguous whether he means a captain of the Salvation Army or the captain of a vessel. The porters rush to my cab, jostling slightly over who will have the privilege of attending to me.

Inside the station, the atmosphere remains bright. The clerk at the booking office greets me with a friendly smile, seemingly pleased with the destination I’ve chosen. He implies that while others may opt for Brighton, a gentleman like me would find this choice more fitting. He takes a moment to highlight that my ticket allows for a return on Tuesday, which, between us, I hope to utilize. In gratitude for his kindness, I slide my newspaper through the pigeonhole. A scruffy little boy thrusts it into my cab; I didn’t ask for it, but today, in the spirit of happiness, I give him a penny.

Following my porter to the platform, he directs me with a casual, “On the left.” This phrase is likely a routine part of his day, yet it transforms into a personal touch directed at me. His tone conveys genuine care; he seems to want me to board the right train, enjoy a pleasant journey, and receive a warm welcome from my friends upon arrival. It feels less like a transaction among a crowd and more like an interaction between individuals.

The porter has secured an empty carriage for me and is brimming with tips aimed at enhancing my comfort. He informs me about the train's departure, the stops we’ll make, and the anticipated arrival time. He inquires if I’d prefer to stow my bag in the van or if I need any newspapers. I decline; I don’t wish to occupy my mind with reading. After handing him sixpence, I feel a sense of shared happiness.

Soon, the guard approaches, and he too seems pleased that I’ve chosen this station from the myriad options available. His satisfaction is not surprising; he expected me to select this route. He assures me that it’s a fine journey ahead, and he wouldn’t be surprised if we enjoyed a splendid weekend.

As I stand at the carriage door, I feel an overwhelming sense of joy. Escaping London feels refreshing, and it dawns on me that all of us are leaving the city for a break from our responsibilities tomorrow. How delightful! Yet I can’t help but think of my porter again. I wish I had offered him more than sixpence. But perhaps he has a sweetheart waiting for him, and that brings him happiness.

The train sets off. I have no reading material, but I’m content to reflect. A railway carriage provides the perfect space for contemplation and joy. I ponder whether I’ll perform well at cricket and tennis, croquet, billiards, and all the other delightful games I plan to enjoy. My gaze drifts outside, landing on children attempting to play cricket in a grimy backyard. I feel a twinge of sympathy for them, trapped in such wretched living conditions. Yet, on this splendid day, their plight cannot overshadow my happiness. I remind myself that they might not envy me; they could very well be happy too. I see them waving, and I wave back.

As my eyes scan the carriage, they settle on my bag. Did I remember to pack everything? Surely I did. Still, an unsettling feeling creeps in, suggesting I’ve forgotten something crucial. I decide to mentally review the evening ahead. Evening attire—yes, that’s packed. Shirts, collars…

I run through my weekend agenda, assigning appropriate outfits for every occasion in my mind. Yes, it appears I have accounted for everything I might need. Yet, I can’t help but wonder if my ambitious itinerary will truly be as enjoyable as I envision. It certainly was last time, as well as the time before that, which is why I’m feeling so elated now.

The train halts at its sole stop, bathed in the warm glow of a September afternoon. A pleasant buzz fills the air as kind, cheerful people greet each other; the atmosphere is one of happiness and contentment. Then we’re off again, and I know my stop is next.

At last, we arrive. A porter greets me with a friendly smile and a casual “Nice day.” I spot Brant waiting outside with the wagonette, which tells me I’m not the only guest arriving on this train. I wonder who else is here. Is there anyone I know? To my delight, I see Bob and Mrs. Bob, and—oh!—Cynthia! And is that old Anderby? How wonderful! I must remember to collect that shilling I owe Bob from our last billiards match. And if Cynthia thinks she can best me at croquet…

We exchange joyous greetings as we climb into the wagonette. The countryside appears more beautiful than ever. "No rain today," Brant assures us, "and the barometer is rising." The porter loads our luggage into the cart and smiles at us. Being a porter can be a tough life, and I genuinely wish for everyone to relish this afternoon. Plus, I find myself short of small change.

I slip half a crown into his hand, and with that simple act, we all share in an even greater happiness.
