As a child, I once encountered a man who embodied the spirit of the Empire—his astracan coat and tightly curled black moustache gave him an air of authority. I often wondered if his moustache was a product of his willpower, allowing him to not only cultivate facial hair but also to inspire a certain presence wherever he went. This man imparted a memorable piece of advice to me: “A man can’t get on nowadays by hanging about with his hands in his pockets.” In response, I jokingly suggested that perhaps success came from having one’s hands in the pockets of others. This prompted him to engage in a discourse on Moral Evolution, indicating that my flippant remark contained a kernel of truth. This recollection, now intertwined with a recent experience, lingers in my mind.

In my life, I have only attempted to pick a pocket once, though it turned out to be my own, perhaps due to a lapse in attention. This act could indeed be described as such; when I rummaged through my own pockets, I was struck by the thrill of discovery, tinged with both ignorance and curiosity about what I might unearth. I wouldn’t claim to be particularly fastidious, yet I can typically account for all my belongings. I know their locations and their histories, provided I keep them from my pockets. Once an item slips into those depths, I bid it a melancholic farewell, akin to saying goodbye to something lost at sea. I suspect the contents of my pockets remain there, akin to the treasures of the ocean, and I regard them with a mix of reverence and uncertainty. There’s a belief that at the end of days, the sea will reveal its secrets; I wonder if my pockets might similarly yield their hidden treasures, although I can hardly recall what they might be, aside from a bit of cash.

This state of innocent oblivion leads me to recount the unusual circumstances that prompted me to empty my pockets. I found myself confined in a third-class carriage for an extended journey. It was evening, though the surroundings were obscured by a relentless downpour, rendering the world outside indistinguishable from the damp wood within the carriage. Without books or newspapers, and lacking even a pencil and paper to jot down thoughts or musings, I was left to reflect on the bare walls and seats. The absence of any printed word or imagery left me with nothing to stimulate my imagination. Ordinarily, even the mere mention of “Sunlight Soap” could spark a cascade of philosophical reflections, but here I was, staring at uninspiring wood, pondering its significance. Just as I began to contemplate the possible reasons for Christ’s carpentry, a realization struck me: my pockets held an unknown bounty. An entire collection of curiosities was at my disposal, and I resolved to explore what lay within.

As I commenced my search, the first item I uncovered was a multitude of Battersea tram tickets. They cascaded from my hands like confetti, enough to host a paper chase. The sight of them stirred a sense of patriotism, bringing tears to my eyes, and they also provided the printed material I so desperately needed. Scribbled on the back of these tickets were brief yet striking scientific essays about some kind of pill. In that moment of scarcity, those tickets transformed into a small, well-curated library of knowledge. If my journey were to stretch on for months, I could envision myself diving into the debates surrounding the pill, crafting arguments for and against based on the information at hand. Yet, it was the symbolic weight of these tickets that resonated most deeply with me. Just as the cross of St. George embodies English patriotism, these scraps of paper represented a form of municipal pride, perhaps indicative of England’s future.

Next, I unearthed a pocket knife. I hardly need to elaborate on the significance of such an item; a knife embodies a fundamental aspect of our civilization's evolution. The very existence of metals like iron and steel sent my thoughts spiraling into a reverie. I envisioned the early humans who first discovered these materials, battling with primitive tools against the unknown. The imagery of a desperate warrior wielding a gleaming weapon against stone axes filled my mind. The sound of hammers striking anvils resonated throughout history, linking the past to the present. The pocket knife, a compact version of a sword, became a symbol of humanity’s primal needs. As I opened it and gazed at the blade, I was reminded of the ancient and formidable power of fire, evoking thoughts of the dangerous yet captivating element that humanity both reveres and fears.

The next discovery was a piece of chalk, which conjured visions of artistic expression and the world’s frescoes. After that, I unearthed a modest coin, bearing the likeness of our own Caesar, a reminder of governance and the order that has prevailed throughout history. The items continued to emerge from my pockets, each one a poetic symbol, yet I lack the space to recount them all in detail. However, one item eluded me entirely: my railway ticket, which remained conspicuously absent amid my eclectic collection.
