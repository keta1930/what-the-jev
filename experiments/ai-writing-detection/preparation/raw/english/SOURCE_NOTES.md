# English human-essay collection

Collected 2026-10-26. Status: 100 complete, locally saved public-domain-era essays. The [manifest](manifest.csv) links each clean body to its author, title, Project Gutenberg source, saved HTML, measured length, natural paragraph count, three block sizes, and SHA-256 hash. The saved HTML files are the unedited source snapshots; `build_corpus.py` recreates all text files and the manifest from them without network access.

Sources: [Stevenson, *Virginibus Puerisque*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/386); [Milne, *Not That It Matters*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/5803); [Gardiner, *Pebbles on the Shore*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10675); [Lucas, *Adventures and Enthusiasms*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/34462); [Chesterton, *Tremendous Trifles*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/8092) and [*All Things Considered*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11505); and Morley's multi-author [*Modern Essays*](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/38280). Project Gutenberg identifies these ebooks as public domain **in the United States**; reuse elsewhere needs a separate jurisdiction check.

The selection contains 100 distinct author-title and text-hash pairs: Chesterton 25, Milne 24, Gardiner 24, Lucas 20, Stevenson 2, and five anthology contributors 1 each. Collection years in the manifest describe book publication, not necessarily the first appearance of an individual essay. For example, the Gutenberg Stevenson text is transcribed from an 1897 edition and the available Chesterton *All Things Considered* from a later edition.

Extraction kept body paragraphs in their original sequence and omitted headings, tables of contents, editorial introductions in *Modern Essays*, illustration captions, Gutenberg notices, and printed page-number markers. One overt fictional fable in Lucas's collection was excluded. Each selected body has 800–1,500 words, at least six natural paragraphs, and a feasible division into three contiguous paragraph blocks containing at least 250 words each. The manifest records the two paragraph cut points and the three block word counts. No article was shortened or combined to meet the limit.

Limits: these are historical published works, mostly from four prolific authors, so they do not represent current student prose. Their circulation online also means they may appear in language-model training corpora. A few period essays use anecdote or satire; they were retained as nonfiction-style personal essays, while obvious narrative fiction and verse were screened out.
