Critics often point out that the barber initiates conversation with remarks about the weather, a practice shared by dukes and diplomats, albeit with a noticeable air of boredom and aloofness. In contrast, the barber engages in this small talk with a remarkable, and indeed astonishing, enthusiasm. Additionally, some take issue with his honesty about hair loss, suggesting that his commendable traits are being used against him. The barber's straightforwardness, stemming from his role as a specialist, is seen as a fault, and his position as a tradesman implies he should be subservient. However, the best way to illustrate the value of a barber's conversation is through personal experience. To avoid any claims of fabricating evidence, I will recount a genuine exchange I had with a barber just a few days ago.

I had received an invitation to an event to meet with Colonial Premiers, and to avoid being mistaken for a rough bush-ranger from Australia, I decided to stop by a barber shop on the Strand for a shave. While enduring the discomfort of the process, the barber made an intriguing observation. 

“There seems to be quite a lot in the news about this new way of shaving,” he began. “You can apparently shave yourself using anything—a stick, a stone, a pole, or even a poker.” I noted a hint of sarcasm in his tone for the first time. “Or perhaps a shovel or a—”

He paused, searching for the right word, and though I was unfamiliar with the topic, I jumped in to help him continue with suggestions that matched his playful rhetoric.

“Or a button-hook,” I chimed in, “or a blunderbuss, or maybe even a battering ram or a piston rod—”

Energized by my contributions, he continued, “Or a curtain rod, a candlestick, or a—”

“Cow-catcher,” I eagerly proposed, and we found ourselves in a lively exchange, each adding to the growing list for some time. Eventually, I inquired about the topic, and he shared his insights with clarity and passion.

“The amusing thing,” he remarked, “is that this concept isn’t novel at all. It’s been a topic of discussion since my youth and well before that. There’s always been this idea that somehow, a razor might be unnecessary. Yet, none of those proposals have ever materialized, and I personally doubt that this one will either.”
